# 08 - Chemistry, chromic inks, reactive consumables and biology as magic methods

Slice: chromic inks and coatings, reactive consumables, classic chemical magic, wetting and fluid
chemistry, smell and taste, and living organisms. Window 2015 to 2026 plus foundations.

**Method note.** The project's shared web-search budget ran out after my first 18 searches, so the
rest of the research was done by opening known primary pages directly (supplier pages, lab pages,
Wikipedia and conservation references). Every URL in "Sources" was either opened or appeared in a
search result. Anything recalled from memory and not confirmed is marked "(unverified)". Prices
are as seen in late 2026 and were not all re-checked for stock.

**Reading guide.** The catalog covers the whole slice as originally briefed. Sections A to D are
grouped by what the chemistry does for a method (hidden marks, self-erasing information, clocks
and self-removing parts, non-visual channels); sections E to G cover classic chemical magic,
fluids and biology item by item; H covers how a hobbyist loads these inks into pens, printers and
print services; I is a practical comparison table (reliability, timing, visibility, cost, shelf
life, mess, hazard, transport). Section 3 (capability primitives) and the level tags (1 = the
phenomenon is the effect, 2 = hidden input to a traditional method, 3 = new method architecture)
were added in response to a mid-project note and are kept as extra material.

## 1. Summary

- **The most useful single item in this slice is sold at every stationer.** Pilot FriXion ink is
  a bistable mark: it goes colourless at about 60 to 65 C and comes back only at about -10 to
  -20 C, so at room temperature it remembers whichever state it was last put in. A salt-ice
  bucket (-22 C), a domestic freezer or freezer spray is a hands-off "developer". Magic has used
  it only for heat vanishes.
- **Thermal receipt paper is a gimmickless secret-writing surface.** It marks from fingernail
  friction alone, so a till receipt, parking ticket or shipping label can be nail-written with no
  nail writer. The object is ordinary and plausibly borrowed.
- **Chemistry gives cheap "delay" and "erase the evidence" primitives that electronics cannot.**
  Conservators' cyclododecane holds parts and then sublimes away in days to weeks with no residue;
  menthol and camphor do the same faster; PVA film and string dissolve in water; cocoa butter
  releases at 34 to 38 C and gallium at 29.8 C; dissolving sweets have been used as real-world
  delay fuses. None of these leave a mechanism to find.
- **Delayed-appearance marks exist for skin, paper and banknotes.** Dihydroxyacetone (self-tanner)
  is applied clear and develops in 2 to 4 hours, lasting 3 to 7 days. Iron-gall ink writes pale
  and darkens in air. A starch mark on a cotton banknote is invisible until an ordinary
  counterfeit-detector pen is swiped over it.
- **Trigger-specific latent images are off the shelf:** body heat (31 C leuco pigment, about $5
  per 10 g), water or condensation (hydrochromic white ink, about GBP 20 per 100 ml), daylight
  (photochromic pigment, cyanotype paper), 365 nm UV (inkjet-loadable invisible inks), rubbing
  (thermal paper, carbonless paper, scratch-and-sniff), and cold.
- **Self-erasing information is practical at three timescales:** seconds to minutes (photochromic
  fade, phosphor afterglow, thymolphthalein ink), on demand (FriXion plus any 60 C source), and
  days (research rewritable papers, 3 to 5 days, not purchasable).
- **Taste is an unused covert channel.** Miraculin (sour reads as sweet for 30 minutes to 2 hours)
  and gymnemic acid (sugar reads as nothing for 10+ minutes) change one person's perception with
  nothing for anyone else to see. They can also carry a cue to one spectator.
- **Research chromics are mostly dead ends for a hobbyist.** MIT's PhotoChromeleon/ChromoUpdate
  needs a lab UV projector and 1 to 30+ minutes per image; rewritable paper, perovskite and
  time-gated security inks are not sold; up-conversion inks need an invisible 980 nm laser.
- **Biology is slow, dim or microscopic, with two exceptions.** Mimosa pudica and Venus flytraps
  can be fired electrically (about 1.3 to 1.5 V, a few microcoulombs) through hidden electrodes,
  and dinoflagellates flash when shaken. Slime moulds, glowing petunias and magnetotactic bacteria
  do not work live.
- **Hazard and transport limits decide what is tourable.** Nitrocellulose is a regulated explosive
  or flammable solid; water beads now have a US federal safety standard; cobalt chloride, the
  mercury-based Old Nassau clock and phosphorus "smoke from fingertips" should simply be dropped.

## 2. Catalog

### A. Latent and bistable marks (conceal, delay, tell)

**A1. FriXion / Metamocolor ink: a two-state mark with memory**
- *What it is.* Microcapsules (2 to 3 microns) hold a leuco dye, a developer and a
  temperature-setting solvent. Pilot states the ink goes invisible above about 60 C and returns at
  about -10 C; Pilot's own history article says 65 C and -20 C. Between those points it keeps its
  last state, which is the valuable property. Pilot warns notes go blank in hot cars and near
  heaters, and that a pen which "stops writing" after heat recovers after freezing.
- *Capability.* A mark that is switched off by any 60+ C source (hair dryer, mug, laminator,
  friction) and on by any sub -20 C source (salt-ice mix at -22 C, freezer, inverted duster spray
  (unverified temperature)), repeatable many times, from a pen costing a few dollars.
- *References.* nippon.com Pilot history; pilotpen.eu range page; McGill OSS explainer.
- *Accessibility.* Off the shelf: ballpoints, fineliners, highlighters, colouring pens, refills.
- *Magic relevance.* Prediction, transformation, vanish of writing. Level 2: outs or a forced
  prediction pre-written and heat-cleared, developed inside a sealed envelope by cold, with the
  performer absent. The spectator can also write with "any pen from the cup" and have their own
  writing vanish later. Tells: a faint ghost and pen indentation on smooth paper; use light
  pressure and textured or patterned stock.
- *Prior use.* Heat-vanish routines exist (Steve Spangler; a "science behind magic" activity that
  hides a message under FriXion scribble and clears it with a hair dryer). I found no source using
  the cold-return direction as a method.

**A2. Leuco thermochromic pigments and liquid-crystal sheets**
- *What it is.* The same chemistry tuned to narrow hysteresis. A standard grade clears at 31 C and
  starts recolouring about 4 C below that (SpotSee/Hallcrest data sheet). Liquid-crystal mixtures
  are narrower and more exact, for example 17 to 23 C or 37 to 40 C, but harder to print.
- *Capability.* An opaque coloured layer that becomes transparent under a fingertip or palm within
  a second or two and re-covers itself within seconds, with no residue. Cold-activated grades do
  the reverse on a chilled glass (unverified thresholds; about 15 C is typical).
- *Accessibility.* Adafruit 10 g pigment about $5; SparkFun 20 g; mixes into acrylic medium or
  screen-print base. High-shear mixing crushes the capsules. Industrial suppliers (CTI, SFXC)
  sell thermochromic, photochromic and glow inks; small-run custom printing needs a quote.
- *Magic relevance.* Level 2: a "blank" or solid-colour panel that reveals what is under it only
  while warmed by the spectator's own hand. Weakness: mood-ring and colour-change mugs are widely
  known, so the layer must not look like a coating. Best hidden as an ordinary black print area.
- *Prior use.* Novelty mugs and cards; specific magic products unverified.

**A3. Thermal paper as a secret-writing surface**
- *What it is.* Leuco dye plus acidic developer plus a sensitiser that melts near 100 C. Any heat
  source marks it, and "a fingernail rubbed quickly across the paper" leaves a mark. Images fade
  in heat, humidity, oils and plasticisers. Developers are often BPA or BPS (about 1 microgram
  transfers in a 5 second touch).
- *Capability.* Write dark, permanent-looking marks on an everyday document with no ink and no
  device. A 3D-printed or brass thumb burnisher gives finer strokes than a bare nail.
- *Accessibility.* Free (receipts). Bluetooth thermal label printers also make convincing one-off
  tickets and labels (pointer to the fabrication agent).
- *Magic relevance.* Level 2: replaces the nail writer on receipts, tickets and labels. The
  writing looks printed-grey, not pencilled, which suits "it was on the receipt all along".
- *Prior use.* None found.

**A4. Hydrochromic white ink (wet and reveal)**
- *What it is.* A porous white coating that scatters light when dry and turns translucent when its
  pores fill with water. SFXC: screen or foam-roller applied, two thin coats, about 50 ml per
  square metre per coat, 100 ml at GBP 19.95; reverts as it dries. Dark underprints show through,
  and one buyer review reports patchy clearing.
- *Capability.* A hidden image that appears only where water lands (a drop, a wet glass ring,
  rain, a damp cloth) and hides itself again in minutes.
- *Magic relevance.* Level 2: the trigger can be the condensation ring from the spectator's own
  cold drink on a coaster or napkin. Tell: the coating is matte and slightly raised.

**A5. Photochromic pigments and light-developed paper**
- *What it is.* Spiropyran and naphthopyran dyes colour under UV or daylight and fade thermally in
  the dark; all fatigue with use. Cyanotype paper prints in "a few seconds in strong direct sun"
  to 10 to 20 minutes on a dull day and is fixed by rinsing in water.
- *Capability.* An image that exists only for a minute or so after exposure to daylight or a UV
  torch, then erases itself (photochromic); or an image formed permanently by daylight through
  an invisible UV mask such as sunscreen (speculative).
- *Research.* PhotoChromeleon (Jin et al., 2019) and ChromoUpdate (Wessely et al., CHI 2021)
  reprogram multi-colour textures with a projector: 60 s for a greyscale preview, about 20 to 33
  minutes for colour, using a LUXBEAM UV projector. Lab-only.
- *Magic relevance.* Level 2 for the fade (evidence removes itself). Level 1 if presented as
  colour change.

**A6. UV-fluorescent, IR and up-conversion inks**
- *What it is.* Invisible fluorescent inks for piezo inkjets (Octopus Fluids for Epson and
  Brother, read at 365 nm on non-fluorescent paper). Up-conversion phosphors (NaYF4:Yb,Er) emit
  green at 530 to 551 nm under 980 nm.
- *Capability.* Print full invisible images, codes or whole marked decks on a home inkjet; a
  Hackaday.io project filled Epson 702XL cartridges to print invisible QR codes for robots.
- *Accessibility.* Inkjet inks off the shelf. Up-conversion pigment is on Alibaba at $1 to $10 per
  gram with a 100 g minimum and needs an eye-hazard invisible laser to read, so skip it.
- *Magic relevance.* Level 2 marking read by a camera (pointer to the sensing agents). Everyone
  knows UV ink, so it must never be the reveal.
- *Prior use.* UV-marked decks are long established.

**A7. Phosphorescent strontium aluminate**
- *What it is.* SrAl2O4:Eu,Dy, about 10 times brighter and longer than zinc sulfide. Honeywell
  Lumilux grades give 270 to 500 mcd/m2 at 10 minutes and 40 to 70 at 60 minutes; the eye's limit
  is about 0.3 mcd/m2. Excited by 200 to 450 nm, so a phone flash or violet LED "writes" on it.
- *Capability.* A surface that stores a light pattern for minutes and then forgets it: a
  temporary, self-erasing display written by a torch, a shadow or a stencil.
- *Magic relevance.* Dark-room and seance work. Level 1 mostly; level 2 as a self-erasing cue card
  readable only by the performer in dim light.

**A8. Delayed-appearance chemistry on ordinary things**
- *Starch and iodine on banknotes.* Counterfeit pens carry iodine: starch paper turns black or
  blue, cotton banknote paper stays yellowish. A clear starch mark on a real note is therefore a
  latent image developed by a pen the spectator already trusts. Vitamin C bleaches the result.
- *Dihydroxyacetone on skin.* Colourless product, colour begins 2 to 4 hours after application,
  darkens for 24 to 72 hours, fades over 3 to 7 days; products run 1 to 20 percent. FDA-approved
  for external skin only.
- *Iron-gall ink.* Ferrous tannate goes on pale grey and oxidises in air to purplish black, so
  storage without oxygen holds it pale (speculative as a method; timing not given in my source).
- *Oxygen indicators.* Mitsubishi's Ageless Eye is pink without oxygen and blue or purple with it,
  slower when cold. Printable UV-activated oxygen-indicator inks exist in the packaging
  literature (Mills, Chem. Soc. Rev. 2005; page returned 403, unverified).
- *Heat, reagent and fume developers.* The classic list (lemon juice, phenolphthalein, iodine
  fuming) is in the Invisible ink reference. Avoid cobalt chloride (IARC 2B, common allergen).
- *Magic relevance.* Level 2 and 3: the display is the spectator's own banknote, skin or sealed
  envelope, and the cause is hours away from the effect.

**A9. Pressure and rub: carbonless paper, pressure film, scent capsules**
- *What it is.* Carbonless (NCR) paper bursts dye microcapsules under pen pressure onto a clay
  developer sheet; self-contained grades put both on one face. Crystal violet lactone is the usual
  dye. Fuji Prescale film does the same in calibrated pressure grades down to "ultra extreme low
  pressure" (5LW). Scratch-and-sniff, a 3M spin-off of carbonless research (Gale Matson, 1960s),
  stores scent for very long periods until rubbed.
- *Capability.* A zero-power copy of anything written, on stationery that looks like a pad,
  delivery docket or form; or a smell released only by the spectator's own rubbing.
- *Prior use.* Impression devices built on carbon and carbonless transfer are standard mentalism
  equipment (specific products unverified). Post-1987 paper is not a skin irritant per a 2000
  review; many grades contain BPA.

### B. Self-erasing information (erase the evidence)

**B1. Off-the-shelf options.** FriXion plus heat (on demand); 31 C leuco layers (seconds, reverts);
photochromic and phosphorescent fade (seconds to an hour); thymolphthalein "disappearing ink"
(blue above pH 9.3 to 10.5, colourless once air acidifies it; time scale unverified, typically
under a few minutes); tailor's air-erasable marking pens (24 to 72 hours, unverified).

**B2. Research inks (not purchasable).**
- Wang et al., Nature Communications 2014: TiO2 plus redox dye plus hydroxyethyl cellulose,
  UV-printed, legible over 3 days, 20+ rewrites.
- Yin group, Nano Letters 2017: Prussian blue plus TiO2, legible 5 days, 80+ cycles, erased at
  120 C in 10 minutes.
- Water-jet rewritable paper, Sheng et al., Nature Communications 2014 (unverified: page
  paywall-redirected).
- University of Michigan 2020: azobenzene layer that forgets a UV-written pattern in about 7 days.
- Ju et al., Advanced Materials 2022: pillar[5]arene and spiropyran ink with tunable fade rates,
  showing a decoy image first and the true one after a set delay.
- Purdue licensing listing: an ink with a 15 minute to 2 hour erase window.
- Perovskite-in-MOF invisible ink, Zhang et al., Nature Communications 2017 (unverified: paywall
  redirect; contains lead).
- *Assessment.* The decoy-then-truth idea in the 2022 paper is worth stealing conceptually: it can
  be imitated with two off-the-shelf inks that fade at different rates.

### C. Clocks, fuses and parts that remove themselves (hold/release, delay, erase)

**C1. Subliming solids: cyclododecane, menthol, camphor, dry ice**
- *What it is.* Cyclododecane (CDD) is a waxy hydrocarbon, melting point 60.4 C, that "slowly
  sublimates over days or weeks without leaving any residue" if pure. Conservators melt it on
  with wax pens, heated spatulas or a spray can (Kremer) to hold fragile objects in transit.
  Menthol sublimes faster and brushes on as a denser film but smells strongly. Heat and airflow
  speed sublimation; foil or polyethylene wrapping stops it.
- *Capability.* A structural spot-weld, spacer or latch that holds for days and then no longer
  exists. Rate is set by thickness, temperature and ventilation, and must be calibrated by
  weighing. It will not sublime inside a vapour-tight container, which doubles as a "safety
  catch": the clock starts when the foil wrap comes off.
- *Hazard.* AIC's 2017 review found too little toxicology to call CDD safe; treat as hazardous,
  ventilate, wear gloves. It can warp polyethylene and polypropylene. Low flash point when molten.
- *Magic relevance.* Level 3. No prior use found.

**C2. Dissolving links: PVA, sugar, gelatin**
- *What it is.* Polyvinyl alcohol is the film on laundry pods, food additive E1203, and is sold as
  dissolving string and bags for carp fishing and as 3D-printer support filament (the last two
  unverified in my sources). Historical proof of reliability: the British limpet mine used a
  dissolving aniseed-ball sweet as its delay because it was "the only substance tested that
  dissolved at a predictable rate".
- *Capability.* A thread, tab or printed part that holds load when dry and is gone seconds to
  minutes after wetting. A whole gimmick can be printed in soluble filament and left in the water.
- *Magic relevance.* Level 3: thread work and loads where the thread cannot be found afterwards.

**C3. Fusible links at body or drink temperature**
- Gallium melts at 29.76 C and is far less toxic than mercury, but wets and stains skin and glass.
  Cocoa butter melts at 34 to 38 C (form V 33.8 C). Coconut oil is lower (unverified, about 24 C).
- *Capability.* A latch that lets go when held in a hand or dropped in a warm drink, with a food
  or metal residue that needs no explanation.

**C4. Diffusion and reaction clocks**
- Time-temperature labels: Timestrip sells single-use liquid-migration labels for 72 hours, 7 days
  and 3 months; OnVu uses a UV-charged spiropyran that fades faster when warm.
- Iodine clocks: delay is set by the thiosulfate or bisulfite dose and shortens with acid, iodide
  or peroxide; a published model exists for the household vitamin C version.
- Swelling: sodium polyacrylate takes up 100 to 1000 times its mass in water, a slow hydraulic
  actuator. Supersaturated sodium acetate (melts 58 C, 264 to 289 kJ/kg) is a one-click heat
  source that can drive any thermochromic mark from inside a prop.
- *Capability.* Cause and effect separated by minutes to months with no battery.

### D. Channels that are not visual: taste, smell, wetting

**D1. Taste modifiers.** Miraculin binds sweet receptors and activates them only in acid; it needs
about 3 minutes of mouth contact, lasts 30 minutes to 2 hours, and is destroyed by high heat or
pH below 3. Sold as berries and tablets; the FDA refused food-additive status in 1974 and the EU
treats it as a novel food. Gymnemic acid suppresses sweetness (sugar, aspartame) with recovery
taking more than 10 minutes; a 2018 study found it also cancels miracle-fruit sweetness.
Denatonium is detectable at 0.01 to 0.05 ppm but carries hazard labelling; do not use on people.
Butterfly-pea extract (FDA colour-additive exemption 2021) turns blue to purple with acid; frozen
into ice it changes a drink as the cube melts. Popping candy holds CO2 at about 50 atm.
- *Capability.* Change what one person tastes for an hour with nothing visible, or give one
  spectator a private signal. Always with informed consent, never covert dosing; ask about
  allergies.
- *Prior use.* "Flavour tripping" parties; magic use not found.

**D2. Smell on cue.** A Seeed Grove ultrasonic atomiser (5 V, 105 kHz, $10.90) makes a scent
emitter from any microcontroller. Aromajoin's Aroma Shooter offers six switchable cartridges over
30 to 60 cm (quote-only). Scent crosses a room in tens of seconds and cannot be cleared quickly,
so it suits one cue per show.

**D3. Wetting differences.** Hydrophobic "magic sand" (silica treated with trimethylsilanol, or
historically wax) is the method of Sands of the Desert, performed in England by 1818 and credited
to Indian magicians in a 1915 Popular Mechanics book. The same idea at mark scale: a wax or
water-repellent pattern on glass or card is invisible dry and shows only under breath or
condensation (speculative; unverified reference). Liquid marbles (water drops coated in lycopodium
or PTFE powder) roll like beads without wetting. Superhydrophobic coatings: pointer to agent 7.

### E. Classic chemical magic and modern upgrades

**E1. Clock reactions (iodine clock, Briggs-Rauscher, Old Nassau)**
- *What it is.* Two clear solutions are mixed and, after a delay, the liquid snaps to blue-black.
  In the peroxide, iodate and persulfate versions the delay ends when a measured dose of
  thiosulfate or bisulfite is used up, so it is tuned by that dose and shortened by more acid,
  iodide or peroxide. A household version uses vitamin C, tincture of iodine, peroxide and starch,
  and has a published model for the switch-over time. Briggs-Rauscher cycles colourless, amber,
  blue about ten times in its usual recipe (dilute mixes gave 40+ cycles in 8 minutes); it needs
  peroxide, iodate, manganese, acid and malonic acid, is spoiled by chloride, and ends with a
  strong iodine smell. Old Nassau goes orange then black using mercury(II).
- *Accessibility.* Vitamin C clock: pharmacy items, cents per run. Briggs-Rauscher: school-lab
  reagents. Old Nassau: do not make.
- *Magic relevance.* Prediction or control ("it will turn when I snap"), sympathetic reaction
  between two glasses. Timing is repeatable to a few seconds only if temperature and volumes are
  controlled, so rehearse at venue temperature. Visible at parlor and stage distance. Not
  drinkable. Reads as chemistry unless the liquid has another reason to be there.
- *Prior use.* Standard chemistry-show items; stage "water to wine" acts are the ancestor.

**E2. Water-to-wine and colour-change liquids**
- *What it is.* The traditional method uses acid-base indicators such as phenolphthalein
  (colourless to pink in alkali; listed among classic developer inks) and thymolphthalein (blue
  above pH 9.3 to 10.5). Not drinkable.
- *Modern upgrade.* Butterfly-pea flower extract is a food colour (FDA exempt-from-certification
  listing, 2021): deep blue, purple with lemon juice, red when mixed with hibiscus. Bartenders
  already use it, and tea frozen into ice cubes changes a drink as the ice melts, which gives a
  hands-off timed change in something the spectator can actually drink.
- *Magic relevance.* Transformation; a drink that the spectator holds and finishes. Cost is
  cents per serving; dried flowers keep for months (unverified). Close-up to parlor.

**E3. Blue bottle and relatives**
- Glucose, sodium hydroxide and methylene blue: colourless at rest, blue when shaken, repeatable
  many times until the sugar is spent. The classic mix is corrosive (about pH 13). A vitamin C
  and copper version runs at about pH 3 with a tenth of the solids. Indigo carmine gives a
  yellow, green, red "traffic light"; resazurin gives blue to pink.
- *Magic relevance.* A sealed bottle that changes colour when a spectator shakes it and "heals"
  when left. Sealed, so no spill risk in performance, but a caustic liquid in a bottle must never
  leave the performer's control.

**E4. Chemiluminescence**
- Luminol: blue (about 425 nm), about 30 seconds per mix, needs alkali (above pH 8.2), peroxide
  and an iron or periodate catalyst. Peroxyoxalate (glow-stick) chemistry is far brighter and
  longer; warmth makes it brighter and shorter, cold dimmer and longer, and a chilled stick
  resumes when rewarmed. Stick contents are irritants and some formulations raised toxicity
  concerns, so use sealed commercial sticks and never open them near people.
- *Magic relevance.* Dark-room productions and "light poured from hand to hand" with sealed
  capsules. Needs low light; parlor at most.

**E5. Foams, gels and instant solids**
- *Elephant toothpaste.* Peroxide (12 percent or stronger), soap and iodide or yeast catalyst;
  foam leaves at about 75 C. Big, messy, stage science only; strong peroxide burns skin.
- *Slush powder.* Sodium polyacrylate absorbs 100 to 1000 times its mass (far less in salty or
  acidic drinks, unverified figure). The standard method for vanishing water in a cup. Non-toxic
  but do not inhale the powder; gel makes floors slippery and can block drains. Pennies per use,
  indefinite shelf life if kept dry.
- *Hot ice.* Supersaturated sodium acetate crystallises on a touch or a seed crystal and warms
  itself; the trihydrate melts at 58 C and is reset by boiling. Lets a performer "pour a solid".
  Reliability is the issue: one stray crystal sets off the whole batch early.
- *Instant-freeze water.* Purified water can be supercooled and freezes when knocked; Coca-Cola
  briefly sold vending machines that did this. Needs a carefully controlled freezer and fails
  often, so it is a video or bar-prep item.
- *Hydrogel beads.* Swell about 100 times and are nearly invisible in water. The US CPSC reports
  about 7,000 ingestion injuries (2018 to 2022) and one infant death; a federal standard took
  effect on 12 March 2026. Keep away from any audience with children.
- *Non-Newtonian fluids.* Cornstarch in water stiffens under impact. Messy; pointer to agent 9.

**E6. Fire and smoke**
- *Flash paper, cotton and string.* Nitrocellulose burns in a bright flash with no ash, igniting
  at about 160 to 170 C. It carries its own oxygen, so smothering does not put it out. It slowly
  decomposes and releases acid in storage; keep it cool, in small amounts, and damp for long
  storage. Magicpedia notes it is sold in sheets (a quarter sheet about 4 inches square is a
  working size) with a hazmat shipping fee, and cites Stuart Robson's 1938 and 1939 tip booklets.
  Transport classes: UN 0340/0341 (1.1D) dry, UN 0342 (1.3C) wetted with alcohol, UN 2557 (4.1)
  for low-nitrogen mixtures. I could not open a TSA page for flash paper (404); assume it cannot
  fly. Low-smoke and sparkle variants exist commercially (unverified).
- *Cold flame.* Demonstrations burn dilute alcohol mixtures at low flame temperature
  (unverified); still an open flame with burn and spill risk.
- *Smoke from fingertips.* The folk method scrapes residue from burnt matchbox strikers, which
  contain phosphorus compounds. Do not use; modern miniature vapour devices replace it (pointer).
- *Dry ice and liquid nitrogen.* Fog and instant freezing; cold burns and asphyxiation risk in
  small rooms, and both are restricted in transport. Pointer to agent 9.

**E7. Metals**
- *Gallium.* Melts at 29.76 C, so a "solid" spoon melts in tea or a hand. It is far less toxic
  than mercury but wets and stains skin and glass. It seeps into aluminium grain boundaries and
  destroys its strength (a drinks can that tears like paper), a process that takes time to soak
  in and leaves contaminated scrap. Bulk price was $120 to $140 per kg in 2016 to 2017; retail
  hobby quantities cost much more. Carriage on aircraft is restricted because it attacks
  aluminium (unverified in my sources). The mercury-free "beating heart" (a gallium drop pulsing
  in acid with an oxidiser) is a lab demonstration with corrosives (unverified).
- *Pyrophoric and thermite-type items.* No hobby-safe version worth recommending.

**E8. Edible chemistry.** See D1 for miraculin, gymnemic acid, butterfly pea and popping candy.
Menthol and capsaicin give cold and heat sensations without temperature change by acting on
skin and mouth receptors (unverified in my sources); a menthol-treated object "feels cold" to
one person, which is a tactile version of the taste channel.

### F. Fluids and wetting chemistry

- *Hydrophobic sand.* See D3. Stays dry under water and pours out dry; pennies per use and
  reusable; the 200-year-old method of Sands of the Desert.
- *Liquid marbles.* Water drops rolled in lycopodium or PTFE powder become non-wetting beads that
  survive gentle pressing and can sit on a water surface; they evaporate in minutes to hours
  (duration unverified). Close-up only.
- *Dancing droplets.* Cira, Benusiglio and Prakash (Nature 2015): droplets of water and propylene
  glycol (ordinary food colouring) on clean glass move, chase and align through each other's
  vapour, even at 1 percent glycol. Millimetre scale; needs very clean glass and still air.
  Close-up or video only.
- *Maze-solving and self-propelled oil droplets.* Lagzi et al., JACS 2010 (unverified: page
  returned 403). Lab curiosity.
- *Marangoni boats.* Soap or camphor at the stern lowers surface tension and drives a small
  float; a camphor boat runs for a long time, a soap boat once per clean dish (unverified).
- *Chemical gardens.* Metal salts dropped into sodium silicate grow coloured tubes in minutes to
  hours (copper blue, cobalt purple, iron orange, nickel green). Several of the salts are toxic.
- *Liesegang rings and Belousov-Zhabotinsky patterns.* Self-forming bands and spirals over
  minutes to days; BZ uses bromate and acid. Installation pieces, not performance.
- *Crystallisation on command.* Hot ice (E5) is the practical version.
- Density columns, index-matched liquids, ferrofluids, Leidenfrost effects, superhydrophobic
  coatings: pointers to agents 5, 7 and 9.

### G. Biology

- **Mimosa pudica and Venus flytrap.** Mimosa leaves fold on touch, shaking, warmth or about 1.3
  to 1.5 V with 2 to 10 microcoulombs, and reopen in minutes; it habituates to repeated harmless
  stimuli for at least 28 days (Gagliano). It grows from seed in 2 to 3 weeks but needs warmth
  (above 13 C) and strong light. A flytrap closes when two hair touches fall within about 20
  seconds; repeated closing can kill the trap. MIT's Cyborg Botany (Sareen and Maes, 2019) fired
  both plants from a mouse click through 30 AWG silver wire. Backyard Brains' Plant SpikerBox is
  $149.99. A 2018 study drove Mimosa leaves with air jets as a display. Sympathetic reaction and
  animation; visible at parlor distance for Mimosa; one shot every several minutes.
- **Dinoflagellates.** Pyrocystis fusiformis flashes blue when agitated, only in its night phase,
  at 19 to 24 C on a 12 hour light cycle. PyroFarms sells 1 litre for $59 and orbs from $89;
  cultures need 2 to 3 nights to settle and are split every 3 to 4 weeks. Shift the light cycle
  to make show time "night". Shipping and cycle changes can suppress the glow. Needs
  near-darkness; close-up to small parlor. No hazard; seawater spill only.
- **Bioluminescent bacteria and fungi.** Aliivibrio fischeri glows blue-green only at high
  population density (quorum sensing), in about 20 g/L salt at 24 to 28 C, and is non-pathogenic.
  Panellus stipticus (eastern North American strains) glows green at about 525 nm from gills and
  mycelium, with a daily peak between 6 and 9 pm, and only on solid substrate. Both are dim and
  need dark-adapted eyes; kit availability unverified. Installation only.
- **Firefly petunia.** Light Bio's plant (about $29 at launch; $17 to $54 listed, some sold out)
  glows continuously "similar to moonlight". Too dim outside a dark-adapted room; cannot be cued.
  US sales only (unverified).
- **Slime mould.** Physarum finds shortest maze paths (Nakagaki 2000), rebuilds the Tokyo rail map
  (Tero 2010) and anticipates periodic events (Saigusa 2008), but over hours to days. It could
  "choose" between oat flakes overnight, so installation or time-lapse only.
- **Instant life.** Brine shrimp ("sea monkeys") and Triops are sold as dried eggs; Triops eggs
  survive 98 C for 16 hours and the young look like adults within 24 hours of hatching. Hatching
  takes a day or more (unverified), so "instant" is a marketing word.
- **Revival.** Selaginella lepidophylla opens a few hours after watering, survives years dry, and
  manages dozens of cycles. Tardigrades revive quickly but are microscopic.
- **Fast germination and living ink.** Cress-type seeds sprout in a day or two (unverified);
  agar art grows a hidden picture over days. A message "grown" overnight is an installation idea.
- **Phototactic and magnetotactic organisms.** Magnetotactic bacteria need a microscope and
  low-oxygen culture. Swarms of light-seeking algae can form visible patterns (unverified).
- **DNA as a prop.** Dead end: nothing visible to an audience, turnaround of hours to days.

## 4. Effect concepts

**1. The freezer prediction (level 2 to 3).**
*Effect.* A card is shown blank on both sides, signed by a spectator and sealed in their envelope.
After the choice is made, the envelope goes in an ice bucket. When they open it, the prediction is
written across their signature.
*Method.* The text is written in FriXion and cleared with a hair dryer beforehand. The bucket is
ice plus plenty of salt (-22 C), or use the host's freezer for ten minutes. The outcome is forced,
or an index of pre-cleared cards covers several outs.
*Bill of materials.* Pens, textured card, salt, bucket: under $20.
*Risks.* Ghost image before development; envelope must stay dry (zip bag "to protect it"); test
whether a pre-heated pen will write invisibly, which would allow writing after the choice.
*Novelty.* I found no published use of the cold direction; moderate to high.

**2. Receipt writer (level 2).**
*Effect.* A total, time or name appears on a till receipt that has been in view.
*Method.* Fingernail or a small burnisher on thermal paper, done under cover of holding it.
*Cost.* Nil. *Difficulty.* Practice only. *Risks.* Stroke quality; some papers are topcoated.
*Novelty.* Not found in magic sources.

**3. The honest banknote (level 2).**
*Effect.* A spectator's note is checked with a shop counterfeit pen; the pen stroke reveals their
chosen card, which was not there a moment ago.
*Method.* A clear starch image is stamped on while handling the note (or the note is given in
change earlier). Iodine develops it instantly.
*Cost.* Pen about $5 (unverified), laundry starch, a carved rubber stamp.
*Risks.* Stamp must dry; marking currency may be unlawful in some places; vitamin C wipe clears it.
*Novelty.* Not found; moderate.

**4. The week-long time capsule (level 3).**
*Effect.* A small cardboard box is sealed with the spectators' signatures and kept by them. A week
later they open it: the folded paper inside has unfolded to show a message, or one card has left
the deck.
*Method.* A cyclododecane spot holds a lightly sprung fold or card. It sublimes through the
cardboard in days and leaves nothing.
*Cost.* CDD from conservation suppliers (price unverified), wax pen. *Difficulty.* High: rate
must be calibrated by weight for the exact box and room.
*Risks.* Timing spread of days; unknown inhalation toxicity, so keep quantities to milligrams.
*Novelty.* High; no prior use found.

**5. Thread that is not there (level 3).**
*Effect.* An object in a bowl of water moves, rises or is released minutes after the performer has
stepped away; everything can be examined.
*Method.* PVA string or film is the only link; it dissolves into the water.
*Cost.* A few dollars. *Risks.* Timing varies with water temperature; slight sliminess.
*Novelty.* Likely new in magic; speculative.

**6. The mark by midnight (level 3).**
*Effect.* At the start of a dinner show a volunteer's wrist is seen clean. Hours later a symbol
has come up in their skin and is still there the next day.
*Method.* A stencilled dab of dihydroxyacetone lotion, applied with full consent as "a blessing",
develops in 2 to 4 hours.
*Cost.* Self-tan lotion, $10. *Risks.* Consent is mandatory since it lasts 3 to 7 days; blotchy
development; symbol must be forced.
*Novelty.* High.

**7. The glass that sweats a name (level 2).**
*Effect.* A cold drink is poured; over a minute the fog on the glass forms the chosen word.
*Method.* A water-repellent pattern on the glass, or a hydrochromic coaster under it.
*Cost.* Under $25. *Risks.* Needs humid air and a cold drink; unverified physics on glass.
*Novelty.* Fogged-mirror messages are old; the drink as trigger is a modest twist.

**8. One tongue only (level 2).**
*Effect.* Two spectators taste the same lemon; one insists it is sweet. Or one spectator "just
knows" which of three cups to choose.
*Method.* That spectator openly took a miracle-berry tablet earlier as part of another phase; the
taste is their private cue.
*Cost.* Tablets about $1 to $2 each (unverified). *Risks.* Allergies, consent, a 3 minute wait.
*Novelty.* High for magic.

**9. The envelope that writes when opened (level 3, speculative).**
*Effect.* A foil-sealed blank card darkens into a message in the minutes after it is unsealed.
*Method.* Fresh ferrous-tannate ink or an oxygen-indicator ink, stored with an oxygen absorber.
*Risks.* Unproven; needs bench testing for contrast and timing.
*Novelty.* High.

**10. Sunlight proof (level 2).**
*Effect.* A blank card held up to daylight shows the prediction for a minute, then it is gone.
*Method.* Photochromic print; the fade removes the evidence.
*Cost.* Pigment about $10 to $20 (unverified). *Risks.* Useless indoors without a UV source.

**11. The plant that flinches (level 1 to 2).**
*Effect.* A potted Mimosa collapses when the chosen card is named.
*Method.* Hidden silver-wire electrodes and a microcontroller in the pot, fired remotely.
*Cost.* About $40 plus plant. *Risks.* Minutes to reset; habituation; plant health.

**12. A presence in the room (level 2).**
*Effect.* During a seance piece, the room slowly fills with pipe tobacco or perfume.
*Method.* Hidden atomiser on a timer or remote. *Cost.* About $20. *Risks.* Slow to clear.

## 5. Dead ends and overhyped items

- **PhotoChromeleon / ChromoUpdate:** lab projector, minutes per image, fades in bright light.
- **Rewritable paper, time-gated and perovskite inks, up-conversion pigments:** not sold, toxic
  (lead) or need a hazardous laser.
- **Printed electrochromic displays (Ynvisible):** 1.5 V, 300 microns thin, EUR 135 a kit, but the
  image holds only 1 to 5 minutes unpowered and reads as "a screen". Custom designs cost EUR 3,500.
- **Glowing petunia, slime mould, magnetotactic bacteria, DNA props:** too dim, slow or small.
- **Old Nassau, cobalt chloride ink, phosphorus smoke, home-made nitrocellulose:** hazards
  outweigh any value.
- **Gallium-crumbled can, supercooled water, dancing droplets:** fussy and slow in front of people.
- **Liquid colour changes in general:** audiences file them under school chemistry.

## 6. Open leads

- Bench-test FriXion: does a heat-cleared pen write invisibly; how visible is the ghost on
  different stocks; how fast does a salt-ice bath develop through an envelope.
- Measure cyclododecane and menthol sublimation rates for 1 to 50 mg spots in cardboard.
- Irreversible and multi-threshold thermochromic inks (a patent family on irreversible leuco inks
  appeared in results but was not read).
- Quotes for small-run thermochromic or hydrochromic printing on cards; MakePlayingCards lists
  spot UV varnish and has no minimum order, but not functional inks.
- Edible printing on wafer paper for gimmicks that are eaten (unverified).
- Galvanic timed releases from the fishing industry as calibrated corrosion fuses (unverified).
- UV-activated oxygen-indicator inks (Mills) and self-contained carbonless stock.
- Whether thermal paper darkens under alcohol hand gel (unverified) as an alternative trigger.
