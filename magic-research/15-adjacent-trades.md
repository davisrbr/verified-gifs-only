# 15 - Adjacent trades and industries: routine know-how that would be a miracle inside a method

Scope: craft and industrial practice (audiology, dental lab, prosthetics/SFX, watch and jewellery,
toys and puzzles, theatre, packaging, security printing, conservation and bookbinding, textiles and
fishing, locks, food, model-making, casino equipment). Academic science and electronics are covered
by the other agents; one-line pointers only where I touch them. All prices are listings seen in
November 2026 and move often. Anything marked "(unverified)" is recalled from memory and was not
confirmed in a source during this pass.

## 1. Summary

- **A hobby-scale packaging line now costs less than one stage illusion.** Deck cellophane sealer
  $75; manual BOPP overwrapper for card/cigarette-size boxes $400-900; handheld induction cap sealer
  $60-375 with liners at $0.10-0.12 a cap; bench can seamer for standard 202 drink cans $140-750;
  shrink bands about $0.02 each; custom "tamper-evident" hologram/void labels with custom serials
  $10-15 per 100. "Factory sealed" stops being a proof and becomes a method, and the seal can be
  applied *after* the secret work, in seconds, sometimes without touching the object.
- **Induction cap sealing is the stand-out primitive in the slice:** it welds a foil liner to a
  bottle mouth *through the closed cap*, without contact, in about 3 s. A bottle can be genuinely
  open during the dirty work and genuinely sealed afterwards with nobody appearing to do anything.
- **The dental lab is the best unexploited hiding place.** An intraoral scan costs $50-150, a
  long-term biocompatible printed splint costs $4-6 in resin, and assistive-tech and military
  products prove a retainer can carry a tongue trackpad (Augmental MouthPad, 7.5 g, 7+ h) or a
  two-way bone-conduction radio (Sonitus Molar Mic). That gives input and output with empty hands
  and *examinable ears*.
- **The thumb tip is 100+ years behind prosthetics.** Life-casting silicone ($60-80 kit), platinum
  skin silicones ($44+), intrinsic pigmenting, scan-to-mould workflows and film "transfer"
  appliances let one person make exact-fit, own-skin-tone fingertips, palm pockets and skin-coloured
  holders. Magic still sells three vinyl sizes.
- **Conservation has adhesives magic has never used:** heat-reactivated reversible film (BEVA 371,
  about 65 C), full-sheet paper splitting with gelatin facings, and cyclododecane, a waxy solid
  that holds things together and then *sublimes away* over days leaving no visible residue. That is
  a zero-part timer and a self-erasing gimmick in one material.
- **Bookbinding plus print-on-demand makes personalised "real" books cheap:** one copy of a
  paperback costs about $4-14, digital edge printing starts at about $1.50 a book, and a single
  leaf can be tipped into a genuine published copy in an evening. Fore-edge painting (an image that
  exists only when the pages are fanned) can now be computed and printed in the page margins.
- **Jewellery services turn a printed gimmick into real hallmarkable silver** (print-and-cast
  services, roughly $200-260 for a pendant-size piece through online bureaux, less through a local
  casting house), and permanent-jewellery pulse-arc welders make a genuinely solid closed loop in
  under a second.
- **Sequential-discovery puzzle mechanics are a library of battery-free state machines**: gravity
  pins, inertia locks, magnet pins, 4- to 60-move sliding sequences. They give "it only opens for
  me" props that survive the "it's electronic" theory completely.
- **Thread: the fishing and fibre trades beat magic-shop thread on strength per diameter by 3-6x**
  on paper (single UHMWPE filaments of 9-30 microns at about 3 GPa), but handling, knotting, creep
  and colour are the real limits. Worth a bench afternoon, not a guaranteed win.
- **Overhyped:** "infrared" contact lenses, magnet-on-eardrum earpieces, tabletop Pepper's ghost,
  latte printers, performer flying on thin lines, cold-cast "metal". Details in section 5.

## 2. Catalog

### 2.1 Packaging and retail sealing

**Deck cellophane and overwrap.**
- *Capability:* re-wrap a tuck box in film so it reads as unopened. Magic-trade route: Pro Deck
  Sealer, $75 with 20 wraps; refill cellophane in 50-packs; a clothes iron on its lowest setting
  also works. Trade route: manual BOPP overwrappers for "poker, cigarette, tea, cosmetic" boxes,
  5-10 boxes/min, 22-30 micron film, 1.5 kW, 13.8 kg (Zonesun ZS-ACW88, $909, listed sold out); a
  generic PID-controlled manual overwrapper at $399; bare-bones units from $42-65 on Alibaba; a
  manual machine with tear tape at 3-5 packs/min (IndiaMART, no price). Fully automatic card
  wrappers are $13,000+ and unnecessary.
- *Custom boxes at quantity one:* MakePlayingCards sells a custom-printed poker tuck from $0.90 to
  $11.35 with no minimum, offers shrink-wrapping and a box seal, and plain white tucks at $0.55.
- *References:* vanishingincmagic.com Pro Deck Sealer and "How to factory-seal a deck at home";
  zonesunautopack.com; makeplayingcards.com wrapping guide and tuck pages; theory11 forum thread on
  resealing.
- *Skill:* an evening of practice. Forum consensus is that the box sticker is easy, the film is
  the hard part; machine-folded ends with a tear tape are what make it read as factory.
- *Primitives:* prove ordinariness; delay (work done days before); brute-force outs (dozens of
  sealed decks, each different, a few dollars each).
- *Prior use:* well established for decks. The overwrap machine, tear tape and non-deck boxes
  (cigarettes, tea, perfume, a boxed product bought "at random") are much less used.
- *Line to respect:* these are the performer's own props. Reproducing a maker's trademarked seal or
  selling re-sealed goods as new is passing off; neither is needed here.

**Induction cap sealing.**
- *Capability:* a handheld head induces eddy currents in the foil liner sitting inside a plastic
  cap; the liner's polymer face melts onto the bottle lip. No contact with the foil, cap stays
  closed, up to 20 seals per minute (about 3 s each). Fits 20-100 mm caps.
- *Cost:* Uline H-8013 $375; generic handheld units $58-222; liners/caps $0.10-0.12 each.
- *References:* uline.com H-8013 and induction caps.
- *Magic relevance:* the only seal in this list that can be made at a distance, through an opaque
  closed cap, after a load. Pill bottles, spice jars, vitamin tubs, sauce bottles all carry these
  seals and audiences know them as untouchable. Primitives: prove ordinariness, delay, erase the
  evidence (the method leaves an ordinary sealed bottle).
- *Risks:* the coil must be within millimetres of the foil (unverified exact gap); metal objects
  near the coil heat; the foil itself gets hot briefly.
- *Prior use:* none known to me.

**Can seaming.**
- *Capability:* a bench seamer rolls a new 202 end onto a standard aluminium drink can body in
  seconds (cycle about 5 s, unverified). Oktober's manual gives the first-operation seam thickness
  (0.074-0.078 in) so a seam can be checked with calipers. Cannular bench seamers $550-750
  (MoreBeer, Midwest), some listings lower; fresh cans and ends are sold to homebrewers.
- *Magic relevance:* a truly sealed, cold, pressurised can containing something that was outside it
  a minute ago. Carbonated liquid or a dry-ice chip before seaming gives the opening hiss
  (pressure hazard: test and under-dose). Primitives: prove ordinariness, conceal, erase evidence.
- *Prior use:* marketed "object in sealed can" effects exist using pre-prepared cans; real-time
  seaming offstage is, as far as I know, not standard.

**Other closures.** PVC shrink bands for bottle necks: $17-22 per 1,000 at Uline, 125 for $17.59
retail, some pre-printed "Sealed for your protection", applied with a heat gun. Impulse heat
sealers for crisp packets and mylar: $37-110 (reseal a snack bag with a factory-width 2-5 mm
seam). Crown cappers, vacuum sealers and blister packs are equally cheap (unverified prices).
Custom-print void/hologram labels with sequential numbers: $10-15 per 100 on stock hologram;
fully custom hologram origination $1,995 (Integraf). The important fact for method design:
**"unique" serial-numbered seals can simply be ordered twice.**

### 2.2 Hearing aids and covert audio

- *Trade practice:* custom shells are 3D-printed from an ear impression or scan (ReSound, Signia,
  Hearing Review). Invisible-in-canal aids sit deep in the canal; Phonak Lyric is placed about
  4 mm from the eardrum by a clinician and worn for months, by subscription (about $3,000/year).
  Broadcast uses purpose-made in-ear prompt receivers (Phonak Invisity, rented by NEP, PRG).
- *Induction:* a neck loop is a copper loop that turns audio into a magnetic field picked up by a
  telecoil (Listen Technologies, Connevans; Williams Sound NKL 001 at B&H). The same physics
  drives the "micro earpieces" sold on marketplaces: listings quote 5x7x10 mm with a 337 cell and
  3-5 h, or a 2x3 mm passive magnet that must rest against the eardrum and is removed with a
  magnet. These are also sold for exam cheating; that is not the use here.
- *What a performer can actually buy:* custom-moulded tips from your own impression or STL for
  $100-200 (Campfire Audio Modulate $189, accepts STL files). A custom skin-toned canal shell
  around a small induction receiver is the reliable version: it seats the same way every time and
  has a removal line.
- *Real detectability:* invisible from the front, visible from the side at conversational distance
  in good light; your own voice booms (occlusion); loops radiate nothing in the Bluetooth/Wi-Fi
  bands. The eardrum-magnet type is weak, medically risky (stuck devices), and not recommended.
- *Theatre practice worth stealing:* sound departments hide 3 mm lavalier capsules at the hairline
  or in wigs with wig clips, milliner's elastic and hand-coloured cable (Shure, ProSoundWeb).
- *Primitives:* tell. Level 2. Prior use: earpieces are long established in mentalism.

### 2.3 Dental lab and things that live in the mouth

- *Capability:* scan $50-150; print a long-term biocompatible splint or retainer for $4-6 material
  (Formlabs Dental LT Clear about $349/L; LT Comfort is flexible); labs charge $100-200 for the
  same part. Custom removable "snap-on" veneers from a mailed impression: about $300-1,000 an
  arch.
- *What has been put in a retainer:* Augmental MouthPad, a tongue-operated Bluetooth trackpad on a
  custom retainer (7.5 g, 7+ h, about $1,400 plus scan, US only, roughly six-month lead time);
  Sonitus "Molar Mic" (ATAC), a clip-on-molar two-way system using near-field magnetic induction
  to a neck loop and bone conduction to the inner ear, tested by USAF pararescue under a roughly
  $10 M contract (2018; later status not confirmed); Tufts' 2x2 mm passive RF tooth sensor
  (Tseng et al., Advanced Materials 2018; lab only).
- *Magic relevance:* hands-free, ear-free input and output. A tongue press is a silent switch
  (advance a cue, fire a release, select one of several outs). Bone conduction is a receiver that
  survives an ear inspection. Level 2 for cueing; level 3 if the whole control surface of an act
  moves into the mouth so both hands are visibly empty throughout.
- *Hobbyist reality:* a printed retainer with a captive magnet moved by the tongue, read by a
  magnetometer in a collar or glasses frame, keeps all electronics and batteries out of the mouth
  (my suggestion; untested). Anything powered in the mouth is a swallowing, aspiration and battery
  hazard and needs potting in certified resin; speech changes with any palate plate and takes
  practice.
- *Prior use:* none known beyond improvised bite switches (unverified).

### 2.4 Prosthetics, SFX makeup, nails, lenses

- *Life-casting and skin silicone:* Body Double skin-safe moulding silicone starter kit $81.61
  (2 lb sets from $59); Dragon Skin FX-Pro platinum silicone from $44; compatible with Skin-Tite
  and medical pressure-sensitive adhesives. Clinical finger prostheses are made by scanning the
  opposite hand, mirroring, printing a mould and casting intrinsically pigmented silicone
  (MDPI Prosthesis 2024; Ottobock's custom line offers six to eight matched tones, colour
  intrinsic so it does not wear off). Hobby precedent: hackaday.io "3D scan to custom soft
  prosthetic hand".
- *Transfer appliances:* thickened Pros-Aide acrylic adhesive (PTM, "bondo" transfers) is pressed
  into a flat silicone mould, frozen, dried and applied like a temporary tattoo with water or 99%
  alcohol; edges dissolve into the skin (Tinsley, US 8,403,766; Koji Ohmura's recipe). Film
  appliances are routinely worn for a full shooting day (unverified duration).
- *What this allows:* (a) exact-fit thumb and finger tips with your nail, your creases, your tone
  and a 0.3-0.5 mm feathered edge (wall thickness is my estimate); (b) a "pocket on the skin": a
  flat pouch on the heel of the palm, forearm or behind the ear holding a billet, coin or tag;
  (c) flesh-coloured holders shaped to the performer rather than to an average hand; (d) hidden
  wiring routed under a skin-tone transfer.
- *Nails:* NFC nail chips with an LED, about 9x11 mm and paper thin, are encapsulated between gel
  layers; 5-packs about 5-8.50 GBP, bulk tags $0.45-0.75. The LED lights with no battery whenever
  the nail is in a 13.56 MHz field. A prosthetic nail overlay hides one completely.
- *Skin printing:* Prinker handheld skin inkjet $170-330 (cosmetic ink, washes off).
- *Contact lenses:* the "infrared lens" kits sold with luminous-ink marked decks are tinted filter
  lenses that raise the contrast of a faint mark; they are not night vision (mechanism from memory,
  unverified). A 2011 Cannes casino case led to prison sentences. In the US every contact lens,
  including non-corrective ones, is a prescription medical device; unprescribed lenses are an
  infection and corneal-injury risk. On light eyes the tint shows. Tinted glasses do the same job
  legally. See section 5.
- *Primitives:* conceal, hold/release, prove ordinariness (empty hands), tell. Mostly level 2.
- *Prior use:* Vernet tips in three or four sizes, soft and hard; novelty "sixth finger" gimmicks
  (unverified). Custom life-cast work appears rare.

### 2.5 Watchmaking and jewellery

- *Mystery clock:* invented by Robert-Houdin (shown 1839), industrialised by Cartier from 1912.
  The hands are fixed to transparent crystal discs with toothed metal rims hidden in the bezel;
  gearing in the frame or base turns the whole disc (Sotheby's, Time and Tide, Christie's lot
  notes). General principle: **move something in the middle of a clear panel by driving the panel
  from its hidden edge.**
- *Print-and-cast:* upload an STL, receive sterling or gold. A comparison ordering one silver
  pendant found $202-260 across Craftcloud, Cooksongold, i.materialise and Sculpteo; printed wax
  patterns on Etsy $20-35; casting-house finishing extras are small (polish $30, karat stamp
  $2.50). Limitation: no enclosed or interlocking parts in one cast (Sculpteo).
- *Micro joining:* benchtop laser welders $5,200-12,400 (Sunstone/Orion), imports from $3,600;
  low-end pulse-arc "permanent jewellery" welders from about $350. A welded jump ring is a solid
  loop with no solder colour.
- *Micro parts:* Lee Spring sells single springs with no minimum in 0.1 mm wire (0.025 in OD);
  Century lists stock wire down to 0.001 in; watch spring bars 1.0-1.8 mm diameter at about $4 per
  ten (Esslinger). These are ready-made micro plungers and detents.
- *Magic relevance:* gimmicked rings, coins, pendants and keys that are real precious metal with
  honest weight, temperature and ring; solid links as final proof; spring-bar-scale latches.
  Primitives: prove ordinariness, hold/release, duplicate.
- *Prior use:* gimmicked coins are a mature craft trade; hand-setting trick watches exist
  commercially (unverified specifics).

### 2.6 Toys, puzzles, automata, paper engineering

- *Japanese puzzle boxes:* himitsu-bako open after a fixed sequence of panel slides, from 4 to 60+
  moves, all-wood mechanisms; $85-250 for traditional boxes, Karakuri Creation Group boxes
  $45-525; free printable 34- and 60-move designs on MakerWorld.
- *Sequential-discovery puzzles and trick locks* add gravity pins, inertia (tap or spin) locks,
  magnet-held pins and tools hidden inside the object (general knowledge of the genre; individual
  mechanisms unverified).
- *Mechanism references:* Cabaret Mechanical Theatre's "Cabaret Mechanical Movement" (levers,
  cams, cranks, ratchets, linkages); Thames and Hudson "Paper Engineering for Designers" and
  Duncan Birmingham's "Pop-Up Design and Paper Mechanics" (V-folds, pull tabs, wheels, dissolving
  windows).
- *Cheap energy storage:* pull-back gearboxes $0.01-0.10 each in bulk (10,000 minimum) or
  $0.12-0.41 used as LEGO parts; clockwork and music-box governors give seconds-to-minutes
  mechanical delays (unverified prices).
- *Magic relevance:* Tenyo has shown for decades that a moulded mechanism can be the whole method;
  Vanishing Inc's blog notes the mechanisms are often cleverer than the effects. A consumer printer
  now makes that class of part at home. Primitives: hold/release, delay, control of choice,
  spectator failure. Level 3 when a prop becomes a battery-free state machine.

### 2.7 Theatre, film and theme-park practice

- *Lines:* stunt riggers use Technora and Vectran braids "as inconspicuous as possible", smallest
  catalogue sizes about 2-2.5 mm; performer flying uses a 10:1 design factor and is not a hobby
  activity. Tabletop loads are another matter (see 2.10).
- *Quick-change construction:* sewn-in PVC-coated magnets (sold explicitly for quick-change),
  large snaps for fast-off, layering ("hard to take a costume off, easy to add one"), tear-away
  garments that leave in pieces in full view. Velcro is avoided for noise.
- *Breakaways:* SMASH! Plastic urethane "glass", 1:1 mix, 5 min pot life, cast in silicone moulds,
  about 75 GBP per 900 g. Shards are blunt but not harmless.
- *Releases:* Electro Kabuki solenoid drops 299 GBP a channel (maker warns it is not fail-safe);
  MagicFX Power Drop $244, holds 20 kg. At tabletop scale the same job is a $2 solenoid or a pull
  thread.
- *Pneumatics:* haunt-industry air cannons $400-485 commercial; Adafruit and Make publish
  solenoid-valve builds.
- *Pepper's ghost:* plain acrylic reflects about 4%; 70R/30T acrylic two-way mirror is sold cut to
  size. Other agents cover optics.
- *Primitives:* release, transform, conceal. Mostly level 2 transfers of backstage practice to
  table scale.

### 2.8 Security printing and document technology

Legitimately purchasable for custom work (not for imitating currency or official documents):
- *Imperceptible whole-surface codes:* Digimarc modulates pixels across the entire artwork in
  repeated tiles so a camera reads the payload from any fragment at any angle; needs 203 dpi or
  better; proprietary. Open equivalent: StegaStamp (Tancik, Mildenhall, Ng, CVPR 2020), 56 bits
  recoverable from a photographed print under perspective, shadow and partial occlusion, code and
  weights on GitHub.
- *Scratch-off:* stick-on scratch panels 40 for about $7; printed cards $2.37 each at 100, toward
  $0.15 at 10,000 (4over4).
- *Invisible inks:* UV-fluorescent refill inks for piezo inkjets (10 ml sets; need 365 nm light
  and non-brightened paper). Near-IR absorbing inkjet inks exist in patents but I found no
  consumer cartridge. Security fibres are sold from 5 kg minimum, so use fluorescent powders or
  inks instead.
- *Desktop UV flatbed:* eufyMake E1 prints colour plus raised texture up to 5 mm on 300+
  materials at 1440 dpi, about $2,300-2,500 (launch 2025). It prints factory-looking graphics,
  fake engraving and tactile marks directly onto finished objects.
- *Holograms and void labels:* see 2.1.
- *Magic relevance:* know (covert identity of any printed surface, read by camera), prove
  ordinariness (props that look officially secured), control of chance (scratch cards with every
  out). The marked-deck and camera agents will go deeper; from this trade's side the point is that
  one-off security-grade print is a retail service.
- *Not found/unverified:* carbonless (NCR) paper as an impression device and thermal paper as a
  heat-developed message are obvious cheap candidates; I did not source them this pass.

### 2.9 Paper conservation, bookbinding, the book trade

- *Cyclododecane (CDD):* a waxy cyclic alkane applied molten or from solvent as a temporary
  consolidant, facing adhesive or water barrier; it sublimes at room temperature. On paper "most
  of the CDD sublimed within several days"; rate depends on thickness, mass per area, temperature
  and ventilation; sealing it in a closed container stalls it; on porous stone it can take four
  weeks or more. Residue-free claims are debated and should be tested per substrate (JAIC 1999 and
  2000; Cambridge repository studies). Melting point about 60 C (unverified). Supply has reportedly
  tightened and conservators also use menthol (unverified).
- *Paper splitting:* gelatin-coated facing papers are bonded to both sides of a sheet and pulled
  apart; the sheet divides through its thickness into two full-size halves, a core is inserted and
  the halves are rejoined (American Antiquarian Society; Library of Congress; JAIC 2000). Magic's
  card splitting is the dry cousin: Blake Vogt's Split Sessions ($4.95) claims a whole deck split
  in under 20 minutes.
- *Reversible heat-set adhesives:* BEVA 371 film bonds at about 65 C with a tacking iron and can be
  reactivated to open again; used for heat-set mends.
- *Fore-edge painting:* an image on the fanned edge of the text block that vanishes when the book
  is squared; "double" and "split-double" versions carry two to four images. Modern equivalents:
  digital edge printing from about $1.50 a book plus setup on 200-1,000-page hardcovers
  (48 Hour Books), and "fore-edge printing" where slivers of the image are printed in the page
  margins.
- *Print on demand:* one 6x9 paperback about $4, one hardcover about $14 (Lulu, no minimum).
- *Tipping-in and rebinding* (replacing a leaf in a bound book with paste on a narrow stub) is
  standard binder's practice (not separately sourced).
- *Primitives:* delay, erase the evidence, conceal (inside a sheet), duplicate, prove ordinariness.
  Level 3 potential is highest here.

### 2.10 Textiles, thread, fishing

- *Measured thin lines:* 8X tippet is 0.003 in (0.076 mm) at about 1.75 lb; 7X 0.004 in at 2.3 lb
  (Orvis, Scientific Anglers). Berkley NanoFil labelled 8 lb measured 0.007 in and broke as high
  as 16 lb (Field and Stream). Braids of 0.06-0.08 mm rated 6-8 lb are reported by anglers.
  Monofilament sewing thread is 0.004 in (0.1 mm), polyester or nylon, 10,000 yd cones (Superior
  MonoPoly, YLI Wonder).
- *Fibre data:* Dyneema filaments are 9-30 microns at about 3 GPa (DSM), up to 43 cN/dtex
  (FibrXL). My arithmetic: a 12 micron filament should break near 35 g and a 20 micron filament
  near 95 g, against perhaps 15-30 g for nylon of the same size. Magic's strongest marketed
  thread (Vectra) claims about 113 g a strand; Kevlar thread is sold as twice ordinary strength
  (vendor claims).
- *Caveats:* UHMWPE is slippery (knots and wax hold poorly), creeps under steady load, and is
  white unless dope-dyed. Stripping a single filament from a $15 braid is the cheap experiment.
- *Fine wire:* 0.1 mm enamelled wire $0.95 for 11 m (Adafruit); 0.05 mm sold by the kilometre;
  AWG 50 (about 0.025 mm) exists from winding-wire specialists.
- *Bonded garments:* Bemis Sewfree films bond seams at about 150 C with no stitch line. Pockets
  and channels can be built into thin stretch garments with nothing showing outside. Tailcoats
  with hidden pockets are old; the new thing is concealment in a T-shirt or fitted shirt.
- *Primitives:* move, hold/release, conceal. Level 2.

### 2.11 Locks and access hardware (own props only)

Hidden RFID cabinet latches cost $6-12 (keyless, invisible from outside, USB or battery); app/NFC
versions $25-72. They are complete, tested, sealed-box latches for less than the parts.
Primitives: hold/release, spectator failure. Too-perfect risk is high (a box that pops open reads
as electronic), so pair with the mechanical locks of 2.6.

### 2.12 Food and drink

- *Printing on food:* edible-ink printer bundles $250-500; foam printers (EVEBOT $1,300-1,700,
  Ripples) print any image on a latte or beer head; chocolate printer kits $1,499 (Cocoa Press).
- *Marking produce:* "natural branding" removes pigment from the outer skin with a low-energy CO2
  laser; works on citrus, avocado, mango, banana; thin skins spoil faster (Photonics Media; Max
  Rubner-Institut).
- *Growing into shape:* pears grown inside bottles on the tree (Alsace, 1700s; still commercial);
  moulded melons and cucumbers. The method does its work months ahead.
- *Custom confectionery:* fortune cookies with custom messages have minimums of 50-100 pieces (10
  boxes at best), not one.
- *Freeze dryers* $1,300-5,000.
- *Magic relevance:* mostly level 1 (the print is the effect) unless hidden in a venue's normal
  service. Sealed-fruit plots gain less than expected: nothing here beats the classic stem-plug
  load. Food safety and allergens apply whenever a spectator eats the prop.

### 2.13 Model-making and replica props

- *Cold casting:* atomised metal powder 1:1:1 with resin, cut back with steel wool and darkened
  with shoe polish; "nearly indistinguishable" from cast metal in brushed or satin finish but will
  not take a mirror polish, and it is warmer and lighter than metal (Smooth-On; Hackaday).
- *Electroforming on prints:* conductive paint (graphite works), copper sulphate bath, controlled
  DC supply; kits $155-250; layer lines plate straight through, so finish first (Prusa blog,
  Make, Formlabs). The result has a real metal skin: cold to the touch, heavier, rings when
  tapped.
- *Surface transfer:* hydro-dipping kits $69-198; UV flatbed (2.8).
- *Primitives:* duplicate, prove ordinariness. Level 2.

### 2.14 Casino equipment (capability pointer)

The Deckmate 2 shuffler carries an internal camera to verify the 52 cards; IOActive showed in
2023 (Black Hat) that this feed yields the full deck order in real time. Angel Eye baccarat shoes
read a proprietary printed code as each card is drawn and hold about 90% of that market. So
"a box that knows every card that passes through it" is 20-year-old shipping industrial practice.
Magic equivalents exist (NFC blank cards $17-117 from Electricks; a 52-card electronic deck at
$550). Other agents own this.

## 3. Capability primitives

1. *Now possible:* seal a bottle with a welded foil liner in about 3 s, through the closed cap,
   with no contact. *Because:* handheld induction sealers, $60-375, liners $0.10. *Before:* glue
   and hope. *Primitives:* prove ordinariness, delay, erase evidence.
2. *Now possible:* seam a new end onto a standard drink can in seconds on a bench tool.
   *Because:* homebrew can seamers, $140-750. *Before:* pre-made gimmicked cans. *Primitives:*
   prove ordinariness, conceal.
3. *Now possible:* overwrap any card- or cigarette-size box in BOPP at 5-10 a minute. *Because:*
   manual overwrappers $400-900 (or a $75 deck sealer). *Before:* iron and patience. *Primitives:*
   prove ordinariness, brute-force outs.
4. *Now possible:* own two sets of identically numbered tamper-evident seals for $0.10-0.15 each.
   *Because:* custom-print void label sellers. *Before:* seals were signatures or wax.
   *Primitives:* duplicate, swap, prove ordinariness.
5. *Now possible:* a rigid, biocompatible, exact-fit carrier on the palate or molars for $5 of
   resin plus a $50-150 scan. *Because:* dental printing. *Before:* nothing lived in the mouth
   except a swallowed load. *Primitives:* conceal, tell, know (input).
6. *Now possible:* receive audio with nothing in or on the ear. *Because:* tooth-borne bone
   conduction (Molar Mic) driven by a neck induction loop. *Before:* earpieces that fail an ear
   check. *Primitives:* tell. (Proven commercially; hobby build unproven.)
7. *Now possible:* a fingertip or skin patch in your exact shape and tone with dissolving edges.
   *Because:* life-casting silicone, platinum skin silicone, intrinsic pigments, Pros-Aide
   transfers; $100-200 in materials. *Before:* three sizes of vinyl. *Primitives:* conceal, hold.
8. *Now possible:* a batteryless light on your own nail that glows only inside a hidden RF field.
   *Because:* NFC LED nail chips, about $1-2. *Before:* thumpers with batteries. *Primitives:*

## 4. Effect concepts

**4.1 Sealed at arm's length (level 3).**
*Effect:* a spectator's signed, folded banknote vanishes. A vitamin bottle that has stood on the
table since the start is opened by the spectator: they must peel the welded foil liner off the
mouth to get the note out.
*Method sketch:* the bottle starts with its cap on and an unsealed induction liner sitting in the
cap. The note is loaded by any classical means (lapped bottle switch, or a load through the open
mouth under cover of re-capping). Afterwards the bottle is set upside-down in a "display stand" or
has a prop box lowered over it; the stand hides the induction head, fired for 2-3 s by foot
switch. The seal is real and was made after the load, without touch.
*BOM:* handheld induction sealer $60-375 stripped into a stand; liners $0.10; bottles.
*Difficulty:* moderate (mains-powered coil in a prop; shielding; rehearsed positioning).
*Risks:* coil-to-foil gap, heat in nearby metal (no coins or foil-strip notes near the coil),
audible hum, burn risk on the foil for a few seconds.
*Novelty:* I found no prior use; the non-contact, through-the-cap property is the new thing.

**4.2 The five-second cannery (level 3, speculative on timing).**
*Effect:* a borrowed, signed object ends up inside a cold, sealed, hissing drink can chosen from
a cooler; the spectator pulls the tab and pours it out.
*Method:* the object is stolen early and passed off. An assistant drops it into an open can of
the right brand, tops with carbonated liquid, seams a fresh end and returns the can to the cooler
by a second route. The can is new stock with a genuine seam.
*BOM:* bench seamer $140-750, blank ends and cans, cooler.
*Difficulty:* moderate; needs an assistant and a quiet offstage spot.
*Risks:* seam quality, over-pressure, motor noise, brand-printed cans versus blank homebrew cans
(shrink-sleeve labels or a house "brand").
*Novelty:* real-time factory sealing rather than a prepared gimmick can; partly new.

**4.3 One leaf different (level 3 architecture).**
*Effect:* a book test done with a current bestseller the host owns. The freely chosen page of the
performer's copy carries a sentence naming the host, the date and the word they are thinking of.
Everything else matches the host's copy.
*Method:* buy the real book. Reset one leaf in matching type, print it on matched stock, and tip
it in on a 3 mm stub in place of the original, trimmed flush. The force is classical. The
personalisation is done the night before for that one audience.
*BOM:* the book, matching paper, laser or pigment-inkjet print, paste, scalpel; under $30.
*Difficulty:* low to moderate bench skill.
*Risks:* paper shade and opacity mismatch; toner sheen against offset ink.
*Novelty:* binder's routine practice, apparently unused as a one-night personalisation method.

**4.4 Two-way fore-edge (level 2).**
*Effect:* a novel is examined. At the end the performer fans the page edges one way and the
chosen word appears across the fore-edge; fanned the other way, the page number appears.
*Method:* print-on-demand book in which software places a sliver of image A in each recto margin
and image B in each verso margin. Squared up, the edge shows speckle. The same marks give the
performer a thumb index to open at any page by feel and sight.
*BOM:* one POD hardcover $14-20; script to impose the margins.
*Difficulty:* low (software and a proof copy or two).
*Risks:* trim tolerance of POD binding (a few tenths of a millimetre blurs the image), so design
bold shapes; the outs must be forced.
*Novelty:* fore-edge painting is 300 years old; computed double fore-edges as outs plus covert
index appear unused.

**4.5 Clean ears, empty hands (level 2 to 3, speculative).**
*Effect:* a mentalism act in which the performer's ears, hands, sleeves and pockets are examined
and they are scanned for radio devices, yet they receive information and trigger events.
*Method:* a printed dental retainer carries a captive encapsulated magnet that the tongue flips
between two or three detents; a magnetometer in the collar reads it (input). Output is by
bone-conduction: the Molar Mic architecture, a neck loop inducing a tooth-borne transducer. The
hobby version of the output side is unproven and may need a powered module in the retainer, which
I would not build without dental advice.
*BOM:* scan $50-150, resin $5, magnet, magnetometer board $10; receiver side unknown.
*Difficulty:* input side moderate; output side hard.
*Risks:* swallowing or aspiration, speech changes, saliva ingress.
*Novelty:* high for magic.

**4.6 Skin pocket and exact-fit tip (level 2).**
*Effect:* any classical vanish or switch, performed with sleeves rolled, fingers spread, both
sides of both hands shown slowly at close range.
*Method:* a life-cast of the performer's hand is used to sculpt (a) a fingertip shell with their
own nail and creases and (b) a flat pouch appliance for the heel of the palm or inner forearm, in
intrinsically matched silicone or as a Pros-Aide transfer with dissolved edges. An NFC LED chip
under a prosthetic nail gives a batteryless cue from any prop containing a 13.56 MHz coil.
*BOM:* Body Double kit $60-80, Dragon Skin FX-Pro $44, pigments, adhesive; about $150.
*Difficulty:* moderate; colour matching is the skill.
*Risks:* edges lifting with sweat; colour shift between daylight and tungsten.
*Novelty:* the upgrade is obvious but, as far as I can find, not commercial.

**4.7 The hold that removes itself (level 3, speculative).**
*Effect (installation):* a sealed glass display holds a folded paper sculpture. Overnight, with
the case sealed and guarded, it unfolds, drops a ring it was "holding", or opens to show a
message. Examination finds paper and glass.
*Method:* the paper is pre-stressed (or a light spring or weight is restrained) by a thin
cyclododecane bond. The bond sublimes over hours to days; time is tuned by film thickness and
ventilation, and can be paused by sealing the object in a bag until the clock should start.
*BOM:* cyclododecane (conservation suppliers; price not confirmed), heat source; under $50.
*Difficulty:* low to build, high to calibrate.
*Risks:* rate varies with temperature and airflow, so the effect has a window, not a moment;
faint residue on some substrates; ventilate when melting.
*Novelty:* I know of no magic use of a sublimating structural adhesive.

**4.8 No-battery state machine (level 3).**
*Effect:* a small wooden chest is locked with no keyhole. Spectators cannot open it; a metal
detector or phone magnetometer app finds nothing active. It opens for the performer, and inside
is whichever of several outcomes is needed.
*Method:* puzzle-box mechanics: gravity pins that free only after a tilt sequence, an inertia
latch that needs a tap in one orientation, sliding keys in order. Different handling sequences
(each a natural-looking way of picking up and turning the box) route to different compartments.
*BOM:* printed or laser-cut parts, steel balls, pins; $10-40.
*Difficulty:* moderate design work; free 34- and 60-move designs exist to adapt.
*Risks:* rattle; a spectator stumbling on the sequence.
*Novelty:* the mechanisms are old; using handling as an index to multiple outs is less explored.

**4.9 Welded shut (level 2 to 3).**
*Effect:* a borrowed finger ring vanishes and is found threaded on a fine chain whose links are
all solid; the chain has no clasp. It must be cut off, or kept as a pendant.
*Method:* the ring is stolen and passed off; an assistant threads it on an open chain and closes
the last jump ring with a permanent-jewellery pulse-arc welder (under a second, no discolouring).
*BOM:* welder from about $350, chain, jump rings.
*Difficulty:* moderate; practise on scrap.
*Risks:* arc flash (eye protection), heat near gemstones, the ethics of borrowed jewellery (keep
the weld on the performer's chain, never on the ring).
*Novelty:* moderate.

**4.10 Duplicated uniqueness (level 2).**
*Effect:* weeks before an event the client receives a parcel: a box sealed with numbered
tamper-evident hologram labels and shrink bands, photographed on arrival. On the night its
contents match the evening's outcomes.
*Method:* every "unique" element (serial labels, bands, overwrap) was bought in duplicate. The
whole box is switched for its twin, loaded backstage and sealed with the matching serials.
*BOM:* labels $10-15 per 100, bands, heat gun, impulse sealer; under $100.
*Difficulty:* low. *Risks:* signatures across seals (control what gets signed).
*Novelty:* an old logic made stronger by retail security printing.

**4.11 Stitched sequencer and the T-shirt topit (level 2).**
*Effect:* a performer in a fitted T-shirt produces, vanishes and switches objects with no jacket.
*Method:* thermobonded internal pockets and elastic channels in a compression-fabric shirt hold
loads flat. A single pull thread sewn as a chain stitch releases several loads in a fixed order
as it unravels, a kabuki drop at garment scale with no electronics.
*BOM:* bonding film, stretch fabric, heat press or iron; $30-60.
*Difficulty:* moderate sewing. *Risks:* load printing through thin fabric; snagging.
*Novelty:* moderate.

**4.12 Edge-driven pointer (level 2; phenomenon close to level 1).**
*Effect:* a clear sealed picture frame holds a single arrow in the middle of the glass, touching
nothing. It turns to the chosen card, hour or name.
*Method:* Robert-Houdin's mystery-clock principle: the arrow is fixed to a full clear disc whose
toothed rim is hidden in the frame and driven by a small gearmotor.
*BOM:* cast acrylic disc, printed ring gear, micro gearmotor; $30-60.
*Difficulty:* moderate (disc reflections give it away unless the frame shadows the rim).
*Risks:* the too-perfect "motor in the frame" theory; make the frame thin.
*Novelty:* low; included as the historical proof that this slice works.

## 5. Dead ends and overhyped items

- **"Infrared" contact lenses with marked cards.** Filter lenses, not IR vision; a prescription
  device everywhere that matters; real eye-health risk from unregulated sellers; visible tint;
  associated with a criminal conviction in the one well-documented case. Tinted spectacles or a
  camera do the same job.
- **Magnet-on-the-eardrum micro earpieces.** Quiet, unreliable, and a genuine medical hazard.
- **Phonak Lyric as a performer device.** It is a hearing aid fitted and replaced in clinic on a
  $3,000/year subscription, not a receiver.
- **Tooth-mounted RF sensors.** Lab-only, need a nearby reader, sense chemistry not commands.
- **Tabletop Pepper's ghost and latte printers.** The apparatus is the audience's first guess.
- **Performer flying on "invisible" line.** Professional rigging with a 10:1 factor; thin line is
  for film and for small props.
- **Cold-cast "metal".** Looks right in satin, but feels warm and light. Electroform or cast real
  metal when the object is handled.
- **Fully custom holograms.** About $2,000 origination; custom print on stock hologram is what a
  hobbyist can afford and is enough.
- **Custom fortune cookies at quantity one.** Minimums are 50-100.
- **Objects grown inside fruit.** Months of lead time for a one-shot prop.
- **Full-sheet paper splitting.** Conservators themselves call it invasive and risky; card
  splitting is the practical version.
- **Single-filament UHMWPE as a drop-in invisible thread.** The strength numbers are real; the
  knotting, creep and colour problems may eat the advantage.
- **Near-IR invisible inkjet ink.** Exists in patents; I found no retail cartridge.

## 6. Open leads

- Bench test: induction-sealer coupling distance and whether a stripped head works through a
  6 mm tabletop.
- Bench test: cyclododecane (and menthol) bond strength and release time versus film thickness on
  card stock, with residue checked under raking and UV light.
- Whether a passive magnet armature on a molar, driven by a neck loop, is audible at all.
- Stripping and handling tests of single filaments from PE braid versus Vectran versus stripped
  nylon; black dope-dyed UHMWPE sources; 0.02-0.05 mm tungsten wire for small-prop suspension.
- Carbonless (NCR) coatings and thermal paper as impression and delayed-writing media.
- POD trim tolerances for computed fore-edge images; whether 48 Hour Books-style edge printing can
  be combined with margin slivers.
- Optician's lab (lens edging, mirror and anti-reflection coating services), philately (regumming,
  postmarks as dated proof), taxidermy and floristry (preserved and dyed organics), vending coin
  comparators as $15 coin-identity sensors, tattoo practice (UV-reactive inks).
- Current status and any civilian release of Sonitus ATAC; MouthPad developer access.
- Induction-sealed foil on non-bottle containers (tubs, tubes, jars with metal lids do not work).
