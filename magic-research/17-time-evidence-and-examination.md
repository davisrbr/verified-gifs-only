# 17 - Time, evidence and examination

Slice: technologies for (a) separating a secret cause from its visible effect in time (*delay*),
(b) making the gimmick remove itself so the final object is honest (*erase the evidence*), and
(c) surviving or exploiting the ways people check that something is ordinary, sealed, untouched or
continuous (*prove ordinariness*).

Prices are as of late 2026 and come from the pages cited. Anything marked "(unverified)" is from
memory or general knowledge and was not confirmed against a source during this pass.

Scope note. This report gives no procedures for covertly opening and re-closing retail
tamper-evident packaging. The prop-building principle that matters is the opposite one (build the
sealed object yourself, around the load), and that is what the examination sections cover. Nothing
here should be used on anything a spectator will eat, drink or take, or on documents, money or ID.

## 1. Summary

- **A bond that ends itself with no residue is now a catalogue item.** Art conservators' volatile
  binding media (cyclododecane, menthol, camphene) are waxy solids that hold, then sublime away.
  Measured lifetimes run from about 1 day (camphene) to about 12 days (menthol or cyclododecane,
  1 to 2 mm blob at 20 C) to months (cyclododecanol). Magic has, as far as I can find, never used
  them.
- **Structural adhesive that lets go on a low-voltage signal is in every iPhone 16.** Electrically
  debonding tapes and epoxies hold at structural strength and release in about 90 s at 9 V, or in
  about 10 s at 45 V. No heat, no sound, no moving latch. The limit is that both bonded faces must
  be conductive.
- **Electronics-free timers with published precision exist in the diagnostics literature.** Sugar
  or wax barriers in paper strips give delays of 44 s to 53 min (variation 11 to 24 percent) or
  1 min to 2 h (about plus or minus 6 percent), and several delays can run in sequence from one
  wetting.
- **Timed corroding links are a $4 fishing-tackle part.** Galvanic timed releases are rated from
  1 day to 7 days and beyond, but only in salt water, and the time shifts about 25 percent for a
  3 C change in temperature.
- **Water-soluble structure is cheap and comes in every form**: film (carp-fishing PVA bags,
  about $0.20 each, gone in 30 s to 3 min), sewing thread, paper (30 s or less), and 3D-printable
  filament (hours). Gelatin is a temperature gate: it swells but does not dissolve below 30 C.
- **Energy and the trigger can be sent into a sealed, battery-free object**: 5 to 10 W through
  12 to 30 mm of tabletop with extended-range Qi, or about 10 to 20 mW from an NFC field, which a
  spectator's own phone can supply. That makes "they triggered it themselves, weeks later"
  technically reachable.
- **Examination is far weaker evidence than audiences believe.** In the Los Alamos seal studies,
  244 seal designs were all defeated, median 43 s, by one person with low-tech tools; volunteers
  comparing a seal impression against a reference were wrong nearly a third of the time; and
  tamper-detection specialists did no better than chance on prepared consumer packages. The
  transferable principles: inspectors have no reference exemplar, they check the feature they are
  pointed at, and a seal's presence is taken as proof.
- **Delay has a perceptual cost as well as a methodological benefit.** Lengthening the gap between
  a false transfer and the reveal from 1 s to 32 s cut the rated strength of the magic by 38
  percent on average. Long delays need a frame that keeps the "before" state alive (a signature, a
  photo, a seal the spectator applied).
- **Identity marks can be machine-duplicated in seconds**, on props: a pen plotter replays captured
  strokes ($430 to $700), a handheld inkjet prints on almost any surface ($110 to $200 at launch),
  and a pocket laser engraves a small mark in about 10 s ($259 and up).
- **Most "self-destructing electronics" are still lab-only.** Dissolvable silicon, depolymerising
  substrates and shattering glass chips are not purchasable. The realistic hobby form of a
  vanishing gimmick is mechanical: sublimable wax, PVA, ice, flash paper, burn-through thread.

## 2. Catalog

### 2.1 Bonds and holds that end on command or on schedule

**Electrically debonding adhesives.**
An ion-conducting adhesive between two metal faces; a DC voltage drives an electrochemical
reaction at one interface and adhesion collapses there.
- ElectRelease (EIC Laboratories) epoxy: 10 to 50 V DC at under 1 mA per square inch; an E3/E4
  example in a patent releases in about 10 s at 45 V and about 20 min at 15 V; release is at the
  positive electrode. A 0.5 square inch bond was shown holding a 650 lb beam.
  Sources: https://www.assemblymag.com/articles/82848-another-type-of-removable-adhesive ,
  https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11081898 ,
  https://discovermagazine.com/technology/introducing-nonstick-glue
- 3M Electrically Debondable Tape 1200-100 (preliminary data sheet, Nov 2025): peel debond in 90 s
  at 9 V, push-out debond in 120 s at 30 V; debonds only between two conductive surfaces. The 1100
  series: under 90 s at 9 to 30 V. https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/p/d/b5005666006/ ,
  https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/p/d/b5005616010/
- tesa Debonding on Demand, believed to be the iPhone 16 battery adhesive: Apple's procedure is a
  9 V battery for about 90 s, faster up to 30 V; older cells take longer; residue cleans with
  isopropyl alcohol. https://www.ifixit.com/News/100352/we-hot-wired-the-iphone-16 ,
  https://www.tomshardware.com/phones/iphone/iphone-16-battery-needs-a-good-zapping-to-debond-the-adhesive-the-process-takes-a-minute-and-a-half-when-using-a-9v-battery ,
  https://www.tesa.com/en/industry/applications/debonding-on-demand
- Fraunhofer IFAM variant needs both 48 V and 65 C, so neither alone triggers it.
  https://www.ifam.fraunhofer.de/content/dam/ifam/en/documents/Adhesive_Bonding_Surfaces/Adhesives_Polymer_Chemistry/debonding-on-demand.pdf
- Accessibility: industrial channels. Hobby routes are replacement iPhone 16 battery adhesive
  strips (unverified that these are sold separately) or a quote from EIC or a 3M converter.
- Magic relevance: *hold/release* at structural strength with no mechanism at all. A joint can be
  pulled on hard by a spectator, then part by itself a minute and a half later. Tells: adhesive
  residue on one face; both parts must be metal and insulated from each other; waits indefinitely.
  No known magic use.

**Thermal-release tape (Nitto Revalpha and similar).**
Heat-expandable microspheres foam the adhesive so it drops to near-zero tack. Nitto lists 120,
150 and 170 C types with a standard release of 100 to 120 C for 1 min; patents on graphene
transfer cite 90 C types as well.
https://www-origin.nitto.com/us/en/products/e_parts/electronic001 ,
https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9828285
Too hot for anything hand-held. Useful only where a hidden heater sits inside a prop.

**UV-release dicing tape.** Peel drops by roughly 10x after a UV dose (patent examples: about
200 g/25 mm before, 10 to 20 g/25 mm after). Weak even before exposure and must be stored dark.
https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8513060
Marginal: a small UV LED behind a translucent wall could release a light flap.

**Volatile binding media (cyclododecane, menthol, camphene).** See 2.2; they double as adhesives.

**Gecko-type dry adhesives.** Setex microfibre tape: shear about 20 to 40 N/cm2 with 90 degree

**Electroadhesion.** 0.7 to 70 kPa at 0.5 to 15 kV, microamp currents; release from under 10 ms to

humidity-sensitive. https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7551419 ,
https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41598-023-38872-6 (pointer: agent 7)

**Ice.** Adhesion to aluminium is typically 0.1 to 0.7 MPa for ordinary ice. A few milligrams of
water as a bond melts, wicks into paper and evaporates: a self-erasing staple with a thermal
trigger. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.03713 , https://www.mdpi.com/2226-4310/9/10/589

**Wringing of flat surfaces.** Lapped gauge blocks hold through a 10 to 25 nm film; one model
puts adhesion near 1 MPa. Needs metrology-grade flatness and cleanliness, so it is a curiosity.
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjsem/14/Special_Issue/14_s36/_article/-char/en ,
https://www.qualitymag.com/articles/83826-wringability-and-gage-blocks

**Burn-through links.** A nichrome wire cuts a tie-down. CubeSat practice: 30 AWG nichrome, about
1.6 A for 5 to 10 s cuts a Vectran cord, with a free wire length of 0.4 to 1.25 in. A sewing
thread or monofilament needs far less (my estimate: under 0.1 J with 40 AWG wire).
https://www.nrl.navy.mil/Media/News/Article/2554563/nrl-invents-cubesat-release-mechanism-to-deploy-solar-panels-tethers/ ,
https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/24810-npo-49969 ,
https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10351269
Tells: a wisp of smell from the melted thread. Flash-string links leave nothing but need care.

**Dissolving bonds**: PVA film and thread, gelatin, sugar, starch. See 2.2.

### 2.2 Matter that removes itself

**Sublimable solids (conservation "volatile binding media").**
Cyclododecane (CDD) is a waxy hydrocarbon, melting at 58 to 63 C, applied molten, from solvent or
by spray, used to consolidate fragile objects temporarily. It leaves by sublimation at room
temperature. Numbers:
- A dense melt-applied CDD film loses about 0.03 mm per 24 h; coatings on glass vanish in 2 to 4
  days; in porous plaster it was still detectable after 9 months.
  https://cool.culturalheritage.org/jaic/articles/jaic38-02-004_1.html ,
  https://cool.culturalheritage.org/jaic/articles/jaic39-03-004_4.html
- 2024 comparison (0.25 g over a 2 cm spot, 1 to 2 mm thick, 20 C): camphene gone in about 1 day;
  menthol and CDD in about 12 days, half that at 30 C; cyclododecanone about 42 days;
  cyclododecanol mostly still there at 100 days. A 75:25 CDD:menthol mix lasted a few days to a
  week. Thermogravimetric rates at 20 C: camphene 216 mg/day, menthol 7.6, CDD 5.3.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10907341/
- Residue: CDD under 0.01 percent of initial mass; menthol can leave menthone on acidic wood.
- Rate depends on thickness, temperature, ventilation and substrate porosity. In a sealed volume
  the vapour saturates and loss stalls, so a sealed prop needs a vapour sink (an activated-carbon
  sachet posing as a desiccant packet is my untested suggestion).
- Cost and supply: conservation suppliers and chemical houses; listings from $7 per 5 g to $230
  per 100 g. https://insituconservation.com/en/products/type/cyclododecane ,
  https://www.fishersci.com/shop/products/cyclododecane-tci-america-3/C0554100G
- Smell: menthol is mint, camphene is camphor-like. Both are tells unless covered (gum, lip balm).
- Safety: camphene and menthol have low flash points; melt in a water bath, ventilate.
- Magic relevance: *hold*, *delay* (days) and *erase* in one material. No magic use found.

**Dry ice.** Sublimes completely. Never seal it in anything; cold-burn hazard. Useful as vanishing
mass. Rates not verified here.

**Water-soluble film, thread, paper and print.**
- PVA film: cold-water grades are defined as dissolving in 300 s or less at 20 C for 1.5 mil film;
  patents target 45 to 150 s at 10 C. Carp-fishing PVA bags, mesh and string dissolve in 30 s to
  3 min depending on temperature and cost about $0.20 per bag; oil slows them.
  https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12291613 ,
  https://www.anglingtimes.co.uk/advice/tackle/guide-to-pva ,
  https://americancarpsociety.com/pva-bags
- Water-soluble sewing thread (Superior Vanish-Extra and Vanish-Lite, Coats Aquamelt): a real
  machine-sewn seam that disappears with a spritz or a rinse; Lite dissolves faster.
  https://www.superiorthreads.com/vanish-extra-water-soluble-thread-spool ,
  https://www.coats.com/en-US/Products/Threads/Aquamelt/Aquamelt
- Dissolving paper (sodium carboxymethyl cellulose; SmartSolve, magic-shop "Dissolvo"): gone in
  30 s or less with agitation, hot or cold. Prints in a laser or inkjet printer.
  https://sites.google.com/meca.edu/materialscollection-rev/home/materials/smartsolve ,
  https://popgoesthepage.princeton.edu/tag/dissolving-paper
- PVA and BVOH 3D-printing filament: hours to dissolve, BVOH faster, helped by 40 C water and
  agitation. https://help.prusa3d.com/article/water-soluble-bvoh-pva_167012
- Gelatin capsules and sheet: do not dissolve below 30 C (they swell); start to disintegrate
  within about 3 min at body temperature. HPMC capsules dissolve the same from 10 to 55 C.
  Enteric-coated capsules took over 2 h in one patent example.
  https://typeset.io/pdf/the-shell-dissolution-of-various-empty-hard-capsules-3avkwxu3ik.pdf ,
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25586554/
- Edible wafer paper (potato starch) prints with edible ink; about $0.25 to $0.60 a sheet.
  https://nycake.com/products/rice-paper-wafer-paper-edible-decorating-sheets
- Prior magic use: Dissolvo billets and predictions (Docc Hilford routines, 1990s).
  https://www.conjuringarchive.com/list/category/421 ,
  https://www.ronjo.com/products/dissolvo-dissolvo-paper-4-full-sheets

**Flash-consumed material.** Nitrocellulose flash paper, string and cotton burn almost instantly
with essentially no ash when fresh and fully nitrated; degraded or low-grade stock burns slowly
and chars. Store damp and cool, small quantities; brown or sticky stock is hazardous; it can ignite
in a hot car. Buy it, do not make it. https://www.vanishingincmagic.com/flash-paper/articles/what-is-the-safest-way-to-store-flash-paper ,
https://www.flints.co.uk/pdf/datasheets/SFXFP01%20Flash%20products%20F.pdf

**Transient electronics.**
- Rogers group silicon-on-silk: magnesium conductors, silicon nanomembranes, silk packaging that
  sets lifetime from minutes to years (Hwang et al., Science 2012). Lab-only.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3786576
- Heat-triggered: wax-encapsulated acid on a depolymerisable substrate, destruction 20 s to a few
  minutes after heating (Park et al., Advanced Materials 2015). Lab-only.
  https://news.illinois.edu/mission-possible-this-device-will-self-destruct-when-heated/
- Light-triggered cyclic poly(phthalaldehyde): monoliths depolymerise within 25 min of sunlight.
  Needs synthesis at minus 78 C. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7047432
- Xerox PARC "DUST": a chip on ion-exchange-stressed glass that shatters to powder within 10 s of
  a light or radio trigger. https://spectrum.ieee.org/us-militarys-chip-self-destructs-on-command
- Soluboard (Jiva Materials): flax or jute PCB laminate that delaminates in 90 C water in about
  30 min. Licensed to manufacturers; no retail route found.
  https://www.theregister.com/2023/07/28/infineon_recyclable_circuit/
- Realistic hobby form: conductive-ink traces on dissolving paper or PVA film, with only a coil
  and a passive part or two. Untested; flagged as an open lead.

**Objects that crumble.** Gallium embrittles aluminium (7075 loses about 60 percent of strength
after 30 min at 75 C; devices painted with gallium-indium "disintegrate within minutes"). Messy,
grey residue, unpredictable timing. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10856174/ ,
https://physicsworld.com/a/liquid-metal-disintegrates-implanted-medical-devices-on-demand/
Stressed glass (tempered sheet, Prince Rupert's drops) fails explosively: eye hazard, not for
close-up.

Notes on the ones that matter most:

- **Paper-fluidic timers.** Dried sucrose in a nitrocellulose or cellulose strip slows wicking by
  raising viscosity (1 versus about 200 mPa.s at saturation). A folding-card device ran four timed
  fluid arrivals (about 2, 6, 13 and 27 min) from one user action. Hobby build: chromatography or
  blotting paper, sugar solution, a wax crayon or wax printer for channel walls. Combine with
  agent 8's colour chemistry for the visible change.
  https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2013/lc/c3lc50178g ,
  https://chemistryworld.com/news/microfluidic-sugar-paper/6216.article
- **Galvanic timed releases.** Two dissimilar metals in one link corrode at a known rate in sea
  water. A one-day link is rated 24 h at 53 F and 18 h at 59 F; a two-day link 48 h at 46 F and

  https://www.nationalfisherman.com/the-longsoaker-promises-to-bait-your-pots-automatically ,
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US11737437

- **Hygromorphs** (wood or wood-filled PLA bilayers) respond to humidity, not to elapsed time, so
  they are a breath or weather trigger, not a clock.
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4383548/ , https://mdpi.com/2073-4360/13/19/3209
- **Osmotic pumps** (Alzet): 100 microlitres at 1 microlitre/h for 3 days; a ready-made slow
  liquid source, lab-supply priced.
  https://www.fishersci.com/shop/products/3-day-mini-pump-1003d-1ul-hr-1/NC0836835
- **Thermal-mass and body-heat timers.** Anything latched with a 30 to 36 C material fires some
  tens of seconds to minutes after it is enclosed in a hand; how long depends on mass and
  insulation, so it must be measured per prop.
- **Creep links, dashpots, evaporation, candle and seed-germination timers**: not sourced in this

### 2.4 Remote and contactless triggers into a sealed object

- **Extended-range Qi through a table.** Standard Qi couples over 3 to 5 mm; NuCurrent's MP-A17
  design reaches 12 mm; under-desk units (InvisiQi) claim 15 to 30 mm at up to 10 W through wood,
  stone or glass, with falling efficiency and failures at 30 mm. A sealed object needs only a
  receiver coil and a resistor to get watts of heat. My estimate: 5 W melts 1 g of paraffin in
  about a minute, a 0.1 g wax latch in well under 10 s.
  https://www.nxp.com/company/about-nxp/smarter-world-blog/BL-QI-WIRELESS-CHARGING ,
  https://www.gadgetguy.com.au/product/invisiqi-solves-a-charging-problem-you-did-not-know-you-had/
- **Direct induction heating of a steel susceptor.** Bonding patents heat a thin susceptor in
  0.05 to 10 s at close coupling. I found no data for hobby ZVS drivers through 10 to 20 mm of
  wood; it needs a bench test. The attraction: the object contains only a steel disc and wax.
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US6639197
- **NFC field.** An ST25DV dynamic tag harvests roughly 4 to 7.5 mA at 2 to 3 V (about 20 mW at
  best) from a reader; phones vary and may be marginal. Enough to charge a capacitor and fire a
  fine burn wire (speculative, needs measurement).
  https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an5233-energy-harvesting-with-st25dvi2c-series-dynamic-nfc-tags-stmicroelectronics.pdf ,
  https://community.st.com/t5/st25-nfc-rfid-tags-and-readers/how-much-power-can-be-harvested-via-stm-nfc-dynamic-tags-e-g/td-p/822426
- **Light through a translucent wall**: the PARC chip used a photodiode trigger; a UV LED can drop
  a UV-release tape; sunlight depolymerises cPPA in 25 min.
- **Palm temperature, drink temperature, breath humidity, water**: gallium, menthol, cocoa butter,
  gelatin (30 C gate), PVA, hygromorphs. All passive, all zero-electronics.
- **Tilt sequences, knock patterns, time since last motion, reed or Hall switches, RF**: pointer
  to agents 14 and 7.

Which make "the spectator did it, in their own hands, long after I left" real? Ranked by how
little they leave behind: (1) body-heat latches (nothing but wax); (2) water or brine links;
(3) NFC-powered battery-free burn wire (their own phone supplies energy and command);
(4) nanopower timers with a motion or orientation condition.

### 2.5 The psychology and practice of examination

**What the research says.**
- Seals: Johnston's Los Alamos (later Argonne) team studied 244 seal designs, half of them in
  high-security use. All were defeated by one person using low-tech methods: mean 1.4 min, median
  43 s; mean first-attack cost $78; devising an attack took a median of 12 minutes. Cost of the
  seal barely mattered (0.3 s more per extra dollar). Electronic seals were not better, partly
  because inspectors trust the reader over visible signs.
  https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/archive/rja14/preprints/Johnston/newpapers/American%20Scientist%20(2006).pdf ,
  https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/efficacy-tamper-indicating-devices
- Inspectors: volunteers shown a genuine seal impression and then asked to spot counterfeits were
  wrong nearly one third of the time, and over-called fakes. Counterfeits were 7.5 to 9.5 percent
  smaller and nobody noticed without side-by-side comparison. Attendees at a tamper-detection
  conference did no better than guessing on prepared consumer packages. (Same article.)
- Consumers: a 1991 Michigan State packaging thesis found every tamper-resistant technology tested
  "failed to some degree to communicate its tampered condition", and that consumers do not know
  how to use such features. https://d.lib.msu.edu/etd/22450/OBJ/download
- Johnston's taxonomy for consumer packaging is that a feature can be bypassed, removed, replaced,
  repaired or recreated, and that a seal can also be compromised before it is ever applied (the
  "backdoor"). For a magician making their own props, only the last idea is needed: the second
  access is designed in at manufacture, so the seal that gets examined is never touched.
- Weight: people detect a change in a lifted object's weight at about 11 to 12 percent.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3222810 (pointer: agent 4)
- Perception does part of the examining for them. Shells are seen as solid objects and the space
  behind an occluder is seen as empty; these impressions are perceptual and survive knowing
  better, and tricks built on them stay hard to work out after three viewings.
  https://boris.unibe.ch/114094 , https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311584 ,
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7339904

**What lay people check, and what they do not** (synthesis; partly from magicians' writing,
partly inference):
- Checked: the feature they are directed to; gross weight; rattle; whether it opens; visible
  seams on the faces presented; whether their own mark is still there.
- Rarely checked: weight to better than 10 percent; balance point; temperature; smell; tap tone;
  wall thickness; the inside of a hinge; the back of a label; whether a "factory" seal matches a
  genuine one (they have no exemplar); whether their signature is the same instance or a copy.
- Never checked: anything that would require destroying the object, or coming back tomorrow.
  That last gap is where time-shifted methods live.

### 2.6 Looking old, used, mass-produced or untouched; impossible locations

Not researched in depth (agents 13 and 15 hold packaging and print finish). Principles:

- **Manufacture around the load instead of re-opening.** Cast, freeze, bake, mould, seam or sew
  the object with the load already inside. Ice (freeze in two stages), melt-and-pour soap, bread,
  candles, resin, plaster and concrete all accept an inclusion; none has a seal to inspect.
- **Natural shells** (walnut, egg, fruit, bulb): the classical methods open and re-close along a
  natural or hidden line (unverified in this pass). What modern tools add: 30 micron resin or
  inkjet printing of a matching shell half; laser-cut closures finer than a knife line; LED bulbs
  with plastic globes that are assembled, not blown; adhesives matched to the substrate.
- **A temporary rigid tool that then disappears**: cyclododecane is used by conservators for
  exactly this (stiffen, handle, let it sublime). It could stiffen a thread, ribbon or folded
  paper for insertion through a neck or a hole and leave it limp and clean days later.
- **Ageing** (tea or coffee staining, UV yellowing, tumbling for wear, liver-of-sulphur patina):
  general craft knowledge, unverified here.
- **Time as a finishing step.** Pressure-sensitive adhesives build strength over a day or more and
  water-activated gums dry out, so a closure made the night before is closer to the factory ones
  than one made at the table (unverified numbers).

## 3. Capability primitives

1. *Now possible:* bond two surfaces so the bond is gone in about 1 day, 12 days or 100+ days at
   20 C, residue under 0.01 %. *Because:* camphene, menthol/cyclododecane, cyclododecanol.
   *Before:* wax and glue stayed forever. *Primitives:* hold, delay, erase.
2. *Now possible:* tune that lifetime by blend (75:25 CDD:menthol gives days to a week) and
   thickness (0.03 mm per day). *Because:* conservation data. *Before:* none. *Primitives:* delay.
3. *Now possible:* a metal-to-metal joint at structural strength that parts 90 s after 9 V, 10 s
   after 45 V, silently and cold. *Because:* electrically debonding adhesives. *Before:* magnets,
   pins, latches with seams. *Primitives:* hold/release, prove ordinariness.
4. *Now possible:* a hold that releases at hand temperature and then evaporates. *Because:*
   menthol (melts 32 to 36 C, sublimes). *Before:* wax left a smear. *Primitives:* release, erase.
5. *Now possible:* a clean, repeatable peel of 0.1 to 0.5 N/cm with no transfer to either face.
   *Because:* fugitive hot melt. *Before:* wax, rubber cement, roughing fluid. *Primitives:* hold.
6. *Now possible:* 20 to 40 N/cm2 in shear with near-zero peel, 1,000+ cycles, no residue.
   *Because:* gecko microfibre tape. *Before:* PSA that fouls. *Primitives:* hold/release.
7. *Now possible:* cut a thread inside a sealed prop with under 0.1 J. *Because:* fine nichrome
   burn wire (CubeSat practice scaled down). *Before:* solenoids, motors. *Primitives:* release.
8. *Now possible:* a real sewn seam that vanishes on wetting. *Because:* water-soluble thread.
   *Before:* false seams. *Primitives:* prove ordinariness, erase.
9. *Now possible:* a film wall or bag that is gone 30 s to 3 min after wetting, $0.20.
   *Because:* PVA carp bags. *Before:* nothing comparable. *Primitives:* conceal, delay, erase.
10. *Now possible:* a link that survives cold water indefinitely but fails in warm water or a
    mouth-warm environment. *Because:* gelatin does not dissolve below 30 C. *Before:* none.
    *Primitives:* release on condition.
11. *Now possible:* 1 to 4 sequential delays between 1 min and 2 h on a piece of paper with no
    power, plus or minus 6 to 24 %. *Because:* sugar and wax paper microfluidics. *Before:*
    clockwork. *Primitives:* delay.
12. *Now possible:* a mechanical release rated in days for $4. *Because:* galvanic timed
    releases. *Before:* clockwork or electronics. *Primitives:* delay (needs brine).
13. *Now possible:* a printed shape that starts moving up to 46 min after it is made, at room
    temperature. *Because:* programmable-onset shape-memory hydrogel. *Before:* none. Lab-only.
    *Primitives:* delay, move.
14. *Now possible:* put 5 to 10 W into a sealed, battery-free object through 12 to 30 mm of
    table. *Because:* extended-range Qi. *Before:* magnets only. *Primitives:* release, move.
15. *Now possible:* have the spectator's own phone deliver both the energy (about 10 to 20 mW)
    and the command to a battery-free prop. *Because:* NFC energy harvesting. *Before:* a
    performer-held remote. *Primitives:* delay, tell, control.
16. *Now possible:* wait months on a coin cell at 35 nA and then act. *Because:* nanopower
    timers. *Before:* weeks. *Primitives:* delay (agent 14).
17. *Now possible:* make your own induction-sealed bottle or seamed can for $50 to $750 of
    equipment. *Because:* small-producer packaging tools. *Before:* re-closing by hand.
    *Primitives:* prove ordinariness.
18. *Now possible:* replay a captured signature in ink on a duplicate card in tens of seconds.
    *Because:* pen plotters. *Before:* steal and load the original. *Primitives:* duplicate.
19. *Now possible:* mark a duplicate object with a serial, initial or sketch in about 10 s.
    *Because:* pocket laser engravers and handheld inkjets. *Before:* pre-printed outs only.
    *Primitives:* duplicate, prove ordinariness.
20. *Now possible:* destroy a printed original in under a second with no ash. *Because:* printable
    flash paper. *Before:* same, but now pairs with machine duplication. *Primitives:* erase.
21. *Now possible:* a rigid insertion tool that becomes limp and clean in days. *Because:*
    cyclododecane as temporary consolidant. *Before:* none. *Primitives:* move, erase.
22. *Now possible:* a gram-scale loss of weight from a vented prop with no residue. *Because:*
    camphene (216 mg/day per small sample) or dry ice. *Before:* liquid drains. *Primitives:*
    transform, erase.
23. *Now known:* a 7 to 10 % size error in a copied seal goes unnoticed without a reference, and a
    weight change under about 11 % goes unnoticed. *Because:* seal studies, psychophysics.
    *Before:* folklore. *Primitives:* prove ordinariness.
24. *Now known:* examination is weakest across time (nobody re-checks tomorrow) and across
    instances (nobody can tell this object from its twin). *Primitives:* swap, delay.

## 4. Effect concepts

**1. The silent walnut (level 3: honest check now, state change later, gimmick evaporates).**
*Effect:* a spectator picks a walnut from a bowl, shakes it by their ear (silent), signs the
shell and keeps it in their fist. A borrowed-looking token vanishes. They shake the nut again: it
rattles. They crack it themselves and find the token.
*Method sketch:* each prepared nut holds a duplicate token fixed to the inner shell with a
rice-grain of menthol. The first shake is honest. Fist warmth takes the shell past about 33 C
and the token drops free. Any trace left on the shell sublimes within days. The token itself is
forced or is one of many prepared outs.
*Materials:* walnuts, menthol crystals (a few dollars), duplicate tokens. About $10.
*Difficulty:* low for the latch, medium for closing the nut invisibly. *Risks:* timing varies
with hand temperature (rehearse with a thermometer; expect 1 to 3 min); mint smell on cracking
(serve with mint tea, or test cocoa butter, which leaves grease instead). *Novelty:* I believe
the time-shifted rattle test is new.

**2. Their phone did it (level 3: spectator supplies energy and command, weeks later).**
*Effect:* the performer leaves a sealed, signed keepsake frame containing a face-down card. Weeks
later the performer phones and asks the spectator to hold their own phone against the frame "to
photograph it". The card is now face up and is the one they are merely thinking of (forced or
prepared earlier).
*Method sketch:* an NFC antenna and dynamic tag inside the backing harvest energy from the phone
into a capacitor; a tiny microcontroller dumps it through 5 mm of 40 AWG nichrome that cuts a
thread; a sprung flap card turns over. No battery, so nothing ages and nothing can be found
"dead".
*Materials:* ST25DV-class tag, low-power MCU, capacitor, nichrome, frame. $25 to $40 plus a PCB.
*Difficulty:* high (energy budget is marginal on some phones). *Risks:* phone-to-phone variation;
the electronics remain in the frame, so the frame is not examinable. *Novelty:* high; speculative
until the energy budget is measured.

**3. Coaster clock (level 3: a disposable, powerless, multi-stage timer).**
*Effect:* at a bar, the spectator's wet glass sits on a paper coaster. Over the next half hour,
answers to things they say later in the conversation appear on the coaster one after another.
*Method sketch:* the coaster is a paper-fluidic network. Condensation or a spill wets a source
pad; four sugar-delayed legs deliver liquid to four hidden colour-forming patches at about 2, 6,
13 and 27 min. The performer steers the conversation (forces) to match the schedule.
*Materials:* blotting paper, wax, sugar, colour chemistry from agent 8. Cents each.
*Difficulty:* medium (calibration, humidity). *Risks:* plus or minus 10 to 25 % timing; needs a
loose script. *Novelty:* high as a method architecture.

**4. Tug-proof link (level 2 to 3: a joint with no mechanism).**
*Effect:* two steel rings, or a chain, are handed out and pulled on hard. Ninety seconds later,
in the spectator's hands, one link opens.
*Method sketch:* a scarf-cut link is bonded with electrically debonding adhesive across an
insulating glue line. A coin cell stack or a contact through the performer's stand applies 9 to
30 V when a hidden reed switch closes. The ring is switched for a solid one, or the gap is
re-closed, before a second inspection.
*Materials:* debonding tape or ElectRelease, rings, cells. $30 to $100 depending on adhesive
access. *Difficulty:* medium to high (sourcing; hiding the glue line). *Risks:* residue on one
face; release time varies. *Novelty:* high.

**5. The table reaches in (level 2: nothing in the object but steel and wax).**
*Effect:* a signed coin is covered; a sealed, examined jar sits on the table. The coin vanishes
and is heard to drop inside the jar.
*Method sketch:* a duplicate coin is waxed under the lid liner with a thin steel disc. An
induction or extended-range Qi coil under the table warms the disc and the coin falls. Before the
effect the jar passes a look and a shake.
*Materials:* under-table coil ($20 to $80), jar, wax. *Difficulty:* medium. *Risks:* coupling
through thick tables is unmeasured; a wax trace remains on the lid (use menthol to remove it over
time). *Novelty:* medium; the duplicate-under-the-lid idea is old, the trigger is new.

**6. Machine-signed duplicate, original burned (level 3: no steal, no load).**
*Effect:* a spectator signs a card. It never leaves view and is finally burned in a flash. The
signed card is found in a sealed tin that has been on the far side of the room throughout.
*Method sketch:* the card is signed on a close-up pad that hides a pen-on-paper digitiser, so the
strokes are captured. Offstage, a plotter replays them on a duplicate card, which drops into a
tin that closes itself. The original is printed on flash paper stock and is destroyed.
*Materials:* plotter ($430 to $700), digitiser, flash paper, tin. *Difficulty:* high.
*Risks:* stroke fidelity, ink match; ethics (cards only, disclose afterwards if asked).
*Novelty:* high; the steal-and-load is replaced by duplicate-and-erase.

**7. The week-long prediction (level 3: gimmick that removes itself in custody).**
*Effect:* a sealed, signed display box is left with a host for a week. On the day, its contents
have changed to match an event chosen that day (or one of several outs has "arrived").
*Method sketch:* inside, a sprung flap or a hanging card is restrained by a pellet of
cyclododecane:menthol blend sized for about 5 days. A carbon sachet labelled as desiccant keeps
the vapour moving. By reveal day the restraint no longer exists, and the mechanism that remains
is a plain paper hinge.
*Materials:* blend, carbon sachet, box. $20. *Difficulty:* medium (calibrating lifetime in that
box at that temperature). *Risks:* plus or minus 50 % timing, so design the reveal for "any time
after day 7". *Novelty:* high.

**8. Brine clock in a souvenir bottle (level 2 to 3).**
*Effect:* a "message in a bottle" of sea water is given as a gift. Two days later the rolled
message has unrolled itself against the glass and reads differently.
*Method sketch:* a one- or two-day galvanic release inside the bottle holds a second scroll
furled; when it corrodes through, a spring opens the scroll over the first.
*Materials:* galvanic release ($4), bottle, brine. *Difficulty:* low. *Risks:* 25 % time shift
with room temperature; corrosion products cloud the water (hide them in "sand").
*Novelty:* medium.

**9. The seam that was really sewn (level 2).**
*Effect:* a borrowed-looking cloth bag is visibly machine-stitched shut around an object and
initialled across the stitching. The object escapes; the stitching is examined and is real.
*Method sketch:* one short run of the seam is sewn in water-soluble thread, the rest in matching
polyester. A damp fingertip opens that run; the hole is closed by a pre-set second line of normal
thread behind it, or the bag is switched. Everything inspected is genuine stitching.
*Materials:* Vanish-Lite thread, about $8 a spool. *Difficulty:* low. *Risks:* sweat or humidity
weakening it early. *Novelty:* medium.

**10. Insert, then go limp (level 3 for impossible-object building).**
*Effect:* an honest impossible object: a knotted ribbon or folded card inside a narrow-necked
bottle, with nothing to find, ever.
*Method sketch:* the ribbon or paper is stiffened with cyclododecane into a thin rod or a tight
pre-folded pellet with shape memory, inserted, positioned, and left for two weeks. The
consolidant sublimes; the object relaxes into a shape that cannot pass the neck.
*Materials:* cyclododecane, bottle. *Difficulty:* medium. *Risks:* slow loss inside a bottle;
leave it open and warm. *Novelty:* medium to high; speculative.

**11. Weigh the thought (level 2).**
*Effect:* a small vented box is weighed on a jeweller's scale by a spectator, who then "puts a
thought in" or "takes one out". Minutes later it weighs measurably less.
*Method sketch:* camphene or a dry-ice chip in a hidden vented cavity loses mass while the box
sits in view. Nothing remains to be found.
*Materials:* scale, box. *Difficulty:* low. *Risks:* smell (camphene); dry ice must never be
sealed. *Novelty:* medium; science-demo flavour, so keep it as a throwaway convincer.

**12. Apply your own seal (architecture note, level 3).**
Since inspectors verify the feature they are pointed at and have no exemplar, the strongest proof
is a seal the spectator applies and signs. Design props so that the examined closure is never
involved: the load path is a second opening built in at manufacture, closed by something that
finishes curing after the load (adhesive that builds overnight, a snap that cannot be re-opened).
Pair with concept 6 or 7 so that nothing needs to pass through any opening at performance time.

## 5. Dead ends and overhyped items

- **Dissolving silicon, cPPA substrates, shattering glass chips**: real, published, not buyable,
  and the last one throws glass.
- **Soluboard**: needs 90 C water for 30 min and is licensed to manufacturers only.
- **Thermal-release tape**: 90 to 170 C. Fine in a fab, useless in a hand.
- **UV-release tape**: weak before release, weaker after; needs a controlled UV dose.
- **Galvanic releases outside brine**: they do not work in fresh water.
- **Gallium on aluminium**: slow, dirty, leaves obvious grey residue, and ruins anything
  aluminium it later touches.
- **Tempered glass and Prince Rupert's drops**: eye hazard at close range.
- **Hygromorphs as clocks**: they track the weather, not the time.
- **Fuses and smouldering delays**: the smell is the evidence, and they are a fire risk indoors.
- **Wringing and electroadhesion as holds**: need metrology-clean surfaces or kilovolts.
- **Electrically debonding adhesive on non-metals**: needs two conductive faces.
- **Long delays on effects that rely on object permanence**: the 38 % drop at 32 s is a warning;
  a delayed effect needs a durable "before" record.
- **Naphthalene, iodine**: toxic or staining; no reason to use them given menthol and CDD.
- **PVA or BVOH printed parts as fast-vanishing gimmicks**: hours, not seconds; film is the fast
  form.

## 6. Open leads

- Measure menthol, camphene and CDD pellet lifetimes inside typical props (envelope, card box,
  jar with and without a carbon sink) and the hand-warm release time of a menthol latch.
- Bench-test heating of a 10 mm steel disc through 10, 20 and 30 mm of tabletop with a cheap ZVS
  driver and with an under-desk Qi unit.
- Measure NFC harvest from current phones into an ST25DV and confirm a burn wire can be fired.
- Find a hobby source for electrically debonding tape (phone repair channels; 3M converters).
- Read the three Michigan State tamper-evidence theses (Sneden 1983, Newartson 1989,
  Iwaszkiewicz 1991) for the actual detection percentages.
- Read Beth and Ekroll (2015) in full for the shape of the decay curve, and look for work on
  whether a signature or photo "refreshes" the before state.
- Conductive ink on PVA film or dissolving paper as a truly disappearing passive circuit.
- Programmable-onset shape-memory hydrogel: can it be reproduced with hobby chemistry?
- Creep links (silicone putty under spring load) and evaporation-through-orifice timers: no
  quantitative sources found in this pass.
- Verify Ortiz and Tamariz references on temporal distance and false solutions against the books.
