# Task 22: the instrumented environment

Report path: /home/user/verified-gifs-only/magic-research/22-instrumented-environment.md

Your slice: **putting all the technology in the surface, furniture and room so that every object the audience sees and handles is passive and ordinary.** Magicians already own one piece of trusted, never-examined infrastructure: the close-up pad (a mat roughly 400 x 300 mm, 3 to 6 mm thick, foam with a fabric top). Add the table, tablecloth, chair, floor, drinks coaster, menu holder, picture frame, lamp, shelf, doorway and the performer's case. Ask what can now be built into each within its thickness and power budget, and what new methods follow.

Lines of inquiry (find more):

- What fits in a 3 to 6 mm pad or a 20 mm tabletop, with numbers.
  - Large-area sensing: NFC/RFID reader antenna arrays and multiplexers that locate and identify many tags at once (board-game and gaming-table research: Microsoft "Project Zanzibar" 2018 flexible NFC + capacitive mat, tangible tabletop systems, RFID poker tables); capacitive arrays through fabric that image hands and objects; pressure-imaging mats (Sensel Morph-class, Velostat matrices, textile pressure maps) that see every object's footprint and weight; load cells under table legs or under the pad that weigh and locate anything placed or removed to under a gram using centre-of-pressure; Hall/magnetometer arrays that track magnets in 3D; inductive coil arrays that detect and classify coins and metal; piezo contact microphones that localise taps, slides and writing by time difference of arrival; optical sensing from below through thin fabric or perforations with IR; cameras looking up through one-way fabric or mesh; radar under the table seeing hands above it.
  - Large-area actuation: planar coil arrays and XY magnet gantries moving objects above (Actuated Workbench, Pico, Reactile, Carl Bugeja flexible-PCB coil arrays; self-moving chessboards such as Phantom, Square Off and GoChess as cheap existence proofs: thickness, force, noise); travelling-wave and vibration fields that slide ordinary objects; hidden air-bearing surfaces through micro-perforated tops; ultrasonic transducer arrays under acoustically transparent cloth; electroadhesion zones that lock a card or coin to the mat on command; electropermanent or rotating-magnet zones that switch a strong hold on and off; heating/cooling zones (Peltier, film heaters) for thermal triggers; induction-heating zones; wireless power zones (Qi and loosely coupled resonant coils powering LEDs or mechanisms in passive props 20 to 50 mm above).
  - Display through the surface: LEDs or e-paper shining through thin veneer, fabric or paper ("hidden-until-lit"); projection from below onto a translucent top; a thermochromic topcoat addressed by a heater matrix so writing appears on the cloth itself; UV LEDs addressing photochromic cloth; magnetically addressed iron-filing surfaces under fabric.
- Thin power and compute for a pad: battery options at 3 to 5 mm, runtime budgets, heat, EMI, weight (a normal pad weighs about 300 to 600 g: how much can be added before a spectator notices?), flexibility (pads get rolled), and how to keep the top surface feeling and sounding like a normal pad when tapped or pressed.
- Furniture and room scale: tables with hidden mechanisms through history (Robert-Houdin's wired stage, séance-table engineering, Losander-style floating tables, thin illusion bases) and modern equivalents at small scale; chairs and floors that sense weight shift, posture and heartbeat (ballistocardiography in chairs: can a chair tell which of five seated people is the one who hid the object, from pulse and fidgeting? report accuracy honestly); floor vibration and footstep localisation; smart lighting as an invisible actor (spectrally tunable LEDs that change how inks and fabrics look, imperceptible strobing as a data or timing channel, barely visible UV-A wash, IR floodlighting for hidden cameras, polarised lighting so a polarisation-coded marking is visible only through the performer's glasses); hidden air currents steering smoke, flames, feathers and bubbles; magnetic fields at table scale (coils in a table edge turning every compass on it, or driving a small magnetised prop anywhere on the surface: field strengths, power, safety around pacemakers and magnetic-stripe cards); installation-scale props (objects on a shelf that have moved when nobody was looking; motion below the perception threshold: how slow is unnoticeable?).
- Portable and impromptu versions: what can be hidden in things a performer can carry into a restaurant or a home and set down naturally (a coaster, a notebook, a card box, a wallet, a drink can, a jacket sleeve resting on the table, or a phone lying face-down acting as magnetometer, NFC reader, vibration motor, reverse wireless-charging coil, speaker or flashlight with no screen ever shown).
- The trust problem: why audiences do not suspect the mat and table, when they start to, and how examination of the surface can be invited (a pad that is itself passive with the electronics in the table beneath; a clip-on unit under a borrowed table; glass tables), plus the historical lesson of stage trick-tables becoming suspect (Robert-Houdin's move to undraped tables).
- Existing magic products that instrument the surface, so you can say what is new: electronic close-up pads and mats (magnetic PK pads, card-reading RFID mats, scale-based "which object" pads, thumper-linked mats), chop-cup-style magnetic tables, any commercial NFC table readers for mentalism. Search dealers.

For each capability give resolution, range above the surface, force, thickness, power, cost and what would betray it (hum, heat, stiffness, weight, EMI on phones). In "Effect concepts" propose 8 to 12 mechanisms where every visible object is ungimmicked or passive.
