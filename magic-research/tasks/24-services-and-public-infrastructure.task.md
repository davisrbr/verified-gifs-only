# Task 24: on-demand services and public infrastructure as method and reveal channels

Report path: /home/user/verified-gifs-only/magic-research/24-services-and-public-infrastructure.md

Your slice: **the world outside the room as apparatus. Which purchasable, API-driven or public services let one person cause something specific to exist or happen somewhere in the physical world, fast and cheaply, and what methods follow?** A prediction that turns up on a billboard, in today's post, baked into a loaf, on a stranger's T-shirt, in a library book, or in a parcel locker across town used to need a television budget and a crew. Find out what it costs one hobbyist now, with real prices and lead times as of late 2026.

Lines of inquiry (find more):

- Programmable physical output at a distance: programmatic digital-out-of-home advertising bought by the minute or by the play (self-serve platforms such as Blip, AdQuick, Vistar-connected resellers, JCDecaux/Clear Channel self-serve where it exists: minimum spend, approval delay, whether creative can be swapped via API within minutes, geofencing to one screen); print-on-demand with one-unit minimums and APIs (Printful, Printify, Gelato, Lulu, Blurb, Moo, Zazzle, Sticker Mule: T-shirts, mugs, posters, books, newspapers such as Newspaper Club, magazines, playing cards, jigsaw puzzles, board games via The Game Crafter, wallpaper, wrapping paper, labels, custom stamps where postal operators allow them, photo products at same-day pharmacy or supermarket kiosks with API ordering); postcards and letters sent by API (Lob, PostGrid, Handwrytten and other robot-handwriting services, Thankster); same-day couriers and task services (ordering a delivery to the venue timed to the minute; ethical use of paid helpers as open collaborators, not deceived third parties); flowers, cakes with printed images, custom fortune cookies, custom M&Ms/sweets, coffee-shop cup printing, pizza toppings spelling a word; engraved or 3D-printed items from marketplaces with rush options; newspaper classified and announcement ads (cost, deadline: the classic headline/classified prediction); radio dedications, cinema pre-roll, stadium screens, bus-stop and taxi-top screens; skywriting and aerial banners; drone light shows by quote; laser projection hire; chalk-stencil and "clean graffiti" services; tattoo-style temporary transfers by post.
- Programmable communication: telephony APIs (Twilio-class voice, SMS, conference calls; real-time AI voice agents that hold a phone conversation: latency and cost; a ringing public payphone or shop phone is out of scope unless owned), scheduled email and messages, shared documents with edit history, calendar invitations, voicemail drops, smart-speaker announcements in the performer's own space, e-ink signage and electronic shelf labels (cheap, API-addressable, look printed), smart-home devices owned by the performer or host with consent, thermal printers online, pagers and retro devices, internet radio, a live webcam or streaming feed of a distant object the performer controls.
- Public records and timestamps as "proof it was there before": scheduled and back-dated-looking content and what really is and is not possible honestly (version-control history, blockchain timestamps and OpenTimestamps, notarisation services, registered post to oneself, newspaper archives, the Internet Archive's Save Page Now, DNS records, app-store or publication dates, domain registration dates, library deposit, patents and trademarks, time capsules, safety-deposit boxes, escrow and sealed-bid services). Explain the structure of multiple-out methods that use many genuine timestamped commitments, and where a method would require deceiving a platform or third party or breaking terms of service (flag these and prefer clean alternatives).
- Places that hold an object for you: parcel lockers (Amazon Locker and carrier lockers: can a spectator be sent to open one?), click-and-collect counters, luggage storage services, hotel concierge and restaurant staff as open collaborators, post restante, self-storage, geocaches, public bookshelves and libraries (placing an owned book), vending machines with a custom slot (rentable or owned), key safes and smart locks with one-time codes, coat checks.
- Data that makes a prediction look impossible but is knowable or controllable: public feeds with predictable or schedulable elements (transport arrival times, tide tables, astronomical events, satellite flyovers and Iridium/Starlink passes, the ISS passing overhead at a named minute, aircraft overhead via ADS-B at this exact moment, sunrise shadows, weather radar a few minutes ahead, live sports data delays between stadium and broadcast, broadcast delays in general, live-stream latency as a time machine), commissioned satellite imagery of a ground marker (tasking prices from the commercial providers; resolution; lead time), mapping and street-view contributions, public sensor dashboards, random beacons (NIST, drand) and lottery draws (unpredictable: say so, and note the classic lottery-prediction methods are late-loading, not prediction).
- Personalisation at speed, with consent: what can be built for a specific named guest between an invitation being accepted and the event (custom book with their name in it, a "vintage" photograph, a newspaper from their birthday via archive services, a bespoke deck with their face, a puzzle whose solution is their word), using information they supply openly. Note privacy lines: do not build methods on scraping people's private data.
- Scale and cost model: for each channel give minimum cost, lead time (minutes, hours, days), how much can vary per order (fully unique per unit?), whether it can be triggered live from a phone in the pocket or a hidden controller, failure and approval risk, and the "out" capacity (how many different outcomes can be staged at once, so a choice made live selects among pre-placed physical outs across a venue or a city).
- Prior art: television magicians' large-scale predictions (David Blaine, Derren Brown, Dynamo, David Copperfield, Penn & Teller, Justin Willman's Magic for Humans), newspaper and lottery predictions, corporate-event custom reveals, and immersive-theatre and alternate-reality-game practice (which has the most developed craft for mailing objects, planting artefacts, staged phone calls and websites: mine it for techniques and costs).

In "Capability primitives" phrase items as "For $X and T lead time, one person can make <specific thing> appear at <place>". In "Effect concepts" propose 8 to 12 routines for a hobbyist performing for friends or small audiences, not a TV special; favour ones with a clear physical artefact the spectator keeps.
