# Task 25: thinness, concealment volume and rapid duplication

Report path: /home/user/verified-gifs-only/magic-research/25-thinness-concealment-duplication.md

Your slice: **the geometry of secrecy. (A) How thin, small and light can functional things now be made, and where is there unused volume inside ordinary objects? (B) How fast and how faithfully can an ordinary object, or its identifying marks, be duplicated?** Concealment and duplication are the two oldest method principles in magic (shells, double-facers, flaps, duplicates, hidden compartments). Both are bounded by manufacturing limits that have moved a long way.

Part A, thinness and hidden volume. Lines of inquiry (find more):

- The limits, with numbers: thinnest practical sheets and films that are still handleable and opaque (metal foils and shim stock down to 10 to 25 microns, Mylar/PET and polyimide films, carbon-fibre veneer 0.1 mm, ultra-thin glass, thin-ply laminates, card-splitting: a playing card is about 0.28 to 0.30 mm and splits into three layers); thinnest shells that survive handling by material (electroformed nickel or copper shells at 50 to 100 microns: electroforming at home over a 3D-printed or wax mandrel; spun and deep-drawn shells; resin-printed minimum walls; vacuum-formed 0.1 mm styrene/PETG; thermoformed films); the classic coin shell and "expanded shell" as the benchmark: wall thickness and fit tolerances achieved by master makers versus what precision turning, micro-milling, wire EDM, chemical etching (photo-etched brass and stainless at 0.05 to 0.5 mm from services such as PCBWay, Photofab-type vendors) and electroforming now offer a hobbyist.
- Flaps, hinges and invisible joins at card scale: living hinges in film, tape hinges, magnetic and electrostatic flap drives, the engineering of flap cards and colour-changing cards, micro-louvre and sliding-shutter surfaces (a face that changes by sliding 0.5 mm: lenticular without lenses, barrier-grid mechanics), stretched-membrane changes, roller-blind and scrolling mechanisms inside 1 to 3 mm, e-paper as the limit case of a zero-moving-part flap (pointer; agent 14), seams hidden in printed borders and pips, tolerance stack-up so a mechanical card still riffles, springs and fans like a card (stiffness and mass targets: a Bicycle card weighs about 1.8 g; what is the detectable difference? pointer to agent 4).
- Dead volume in ordinary objects: survey of where space exists inside things audiences accept (pens and markers such as the Sharpie, lighters, lip balm, card boxes with their flap and cellophane, wallets, phone cases, coins as stacks, bottle caps, drink cans with double walls or false bottoms, books and hollowed pads, watch cases, rings, glasses frames, shoes, belts, buttons, zipper pulls, earbud cases, key fobs, battery cases as dummy batteries, USB sticks, dice with 16 mm of volume, matchboxes, tea bags, sugar sachets, chewing-gum packs). For each: usable internal volume and dimensions, what can be fitted from agents 7 and 14's parts (pointer only), and whether an X-ray-free inspection (weight, balance, rattle, flex, transparency against a light) would expose it. Industrial CT scans of consumer products published online (Lumafield's public scans and similar) are a good source for internal layouts.
- Hiding in plain sight on the body and table: flesh-tone and surface-matched covers (pointer; agent 15), colour and texture matching by spectrophotometer and custom paint mixing, printed camouflage matched to a specific table or cloth from a photo (print the pattern of the surface onto a cover: a "shell" of the tabletop itself, the black-art principle generalised to any background with UV printing), ultra-black cavities, thin mirrors (front-surface acrylic and film mirrors at 0.1 to 1 mm for miniature mirror boxes and mirror glasses), edge treatments that hide thickness (chamfers, blackened edges), active camouflage in research (cephalopod-inspired colour-changing skins, e-ink "chameleon" surfaces) with an honest readiness assessment.
- Compaction: how much can come out of how little (pointer; agent 27 covers deployables in depth).

Part B, rapid duplication. Lines of inquiry:

- Shape: phone photogrammetry and LiDAR, structured-light desktop scanners (Revopoint, Creality, Einstar class: accuracy 0.02 to 0.1 mm), turnaround from scan to a printed, finished duplicate, colour and finish matching (multi-colour FDM, full-colour services, hydro-dipping, UV printing, plating, cold casting with metal powders), weight matching with dense fills. What is the fastest credible pipeline for "an object I saw yesterday" and for "an object handed to me ten minutes ago backstage"? What is achievable with a portable kit at a venue (small resin printer, UV printer, mould putty and fast-cast resin: silicone putty moulds set in 10 to 20 minutes, 3-minute polyurethane)?
- Surface and print: duplicating a printed item (a specific playing card with wear, a business card, a label, a ticket, a page) by scan and print: scanner resolution, colour management, stock matching, edge cutting with a craft cutter, portable printers (pocket inkjet, thermal, dye-sub) and their quality; transferring an image or mark from one surface to another quickly (solvent/toner transfer, heat transfer, water-slide decal, cold-peel film, rub-down transfers, blender pens, gel medium, laser-toner foil, stamp making in minutes with photopolymer or laser-engraved rubber).
- Identity marks: the standard proofs of identity in magic are a signature, a torn corner, a serial number, an initialled sticker, a spectator's drawing. Survey the technical ways such a mark can be reproduced or transferred onto a duplicate within seconds to minutes under performance conditions for theatre on playing cards and similar props (concealed small plotters or printers, pre-made sets of all likely marks, torn-corner matching by pre-torn duplicates and by fabrication: a tear line cut by laser or programmed weakening so two cards tear identically; photographic capture and reprint), and their limits. Do not give guidance for forging signatures on documents, cheques, IDs, tickets with monetary value or currency; say plainly where the legal line is (currency reproduction rules, trademark and counterfeit goods rules) and keep to props.
- Self-similarity instead of duplication: making the "unique" object not unique in advance (mass-produced "one-off" items, pre-distributed identical items among an audience, objects whose apparent random damage is reproducible: laser-scored tear lines, moulded "broken" edges, printed "handwriting", controlled crumpling by scoring), and designing a mark procedure so that the spectator's mark is constrained without their noticing.
- Detection: how do experts and lay people tell a duplicate (weight, sound, feel, print dots under a loupe, UV response, edge colour, smell)? Which of these would an audience ever apply?

Give numbers, suppliers and prices throughout. In "Effect concepts" propose 8 to 12 mechanisms, with at least four that depend on a thinness or duplication capability that did not exist ten years ago.
