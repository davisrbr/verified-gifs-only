# Task 27: speed and compaction

Report path: /home/user/verified-gifs-only/magic-research/27-speed-and-compaction.md

Your slice: **two physical quantities that bound productions, vanishes and transformations: (A) how fast a thing can be made to move, change or get out of sight, relative to what human vision can follow; (B) how small a thing can be stowed relative to its deployed size, and how quickly and quietly it deploys or collapses.** Classical magic has springs, elastic, feather flowers, appearing canes, silks and flash paper. Engineering has a large literature on both quantities that magic has not read.

Part A, speed. Lines of inquiry (find more):

- The target, from vision science with numbers: at what speed, duration and contrast does a moving object stop being seen as a moving object (motion smear, the roughly 20 to 100 ms windows around saccades and blinks, flicker fusion, the limits of smooth pursuit at about 30 to 100 degrees per second, apparent-motion and change-blindness conditions, masking by a flash or a larger motion). Turn this into a design table: for a coin, card, ball or silk at 0.5 m and at 3 m viewing distance, how fast and how far must it move to "vanish", and how short must a surface change be to read as instantaneous? Note the difference between eyes and cameras (frame rate, rolling shutter) since performances are filmed.
- Fast mechanisms at prop scale: latch-mediated spring actuation (Longo, Patek et al. 2019 "Beyond power amplification: latch-mediated spring actuation"; engineered jumpers and strikers; Ilton et al. 2018 Science on the principles; Hawkes et al. 2022 Nature jumper), snap-through bistable shells and tape springs, elastic and constant-force springs, pre-stretched elastomer and silicone bands versus classic magicians' elastic (energy density, creep, life), compressed gas micro-cylinders and CO2 cartridges, pyrotechnic-free "airbag-like" inflation, voice coils and solenoids (peak accelerations), piezo stack and amplified piezo, electromagnetic launch (coil and reluctance at coin scale), electro-permanent release for zero-latency triggering, SMA is slow (say so), combustion-free fast shutters (camera focal-plane shutters travel at several m/s and are tiny and cheap as spares; leaf shutters; LCD and PDLC shutters with ms switching; MEMS and galvo mirrors; fast roller blinds; high-speed doors), flip-dot and split-flap speeds, iris mechanisms, how to stop fast without noise (eddy-current and viscous dampers, sorbothane, crush zones) and how to cover or eliminate the sound of release. Give accelerations, travel times over 50 to 500 mm, stored energy, noise in dB, size, and safety (anything moving at 10+ m/s near faces).
- Fast surface change with little or no motion: electro-optic (PDLC, electrochromic is slow, liquid-crystal light valves, electrophoretic e-paper refresh times, electrowetting displays: fast and reflective), flash-bleaching and photochromic speed, thermochromic response time for thin films on low thermal mass, polariser rotation, mechanical micro-shutter and louvre arrays that change a surface with sub-millimetre travel, stretched or peeled membranes, combustion (flash paper and flash cotton burn times; cold alternatives), sublimation and puff (a film blown off by a gas pulse), magnetic flip of bistable flakes (magnetic-paint and flip-dot at micro scale, Gyricon/rotating-ball paper).
- Timing aids: releasing on the performer's own off-beat or on the audience's blink or saccade (blink synchronisation studies in audiences; triggering from a detected loud sound, laugh or flash), covering motion with a larger motion (kinematic masking), and what sensing would let a system time itself better than a person can (pointer; agent 10).

Part B, compaction. Lines of inquiry:

- Deployable-structure engineering, with stow ratios: origami patterns (Miura-ori, flasher, Kresling, waterbomb, Yoshimura; thick origami; the origami starshade and solar-array literature for 10:1 to 30:1 diameter ratios), tape-spring and bistable composite booms (STEM, TRAC, CTM booms and consumer tape measures; bistable reeled composites such as RolaTube: metres of rigid pole from a hand-sized coil, the engineered descendant of the appearing cane), telescoping structures (computational telescoping of Yu et al. 2017; antenna masts), scissor and Hoberman mechanisms, tensegrity and tension-membrane deployables, coilable masts, inflatable structures (drop-stitch fabric that becomes rigid at low pressure; air beams; inflation by micro-pump, by chemical gas generation from citric acid and bicarbonate, by CO2 bulb; deflation speed for vanishes), spring steel pop-up hoops (the pop-up tent and photographic reflector principle: 3:1 diameter fold), auxetic and kirigami expansion, compressed foams and sponges (memory foam compression ratios and recovery times, cellulose sponge expanding in water, vacuum-packed foam objects that look solid when recovered: compression ratio 10:1 and more), spring-wire forms (classic spring flowers, spring animals), feather and silk as the classical benchmarks (packing volume of a 45 cm silk; modern ultra-light fabrics: 5 to 7 denier ripstop, parachute silk substitutes, mylar and holographic films, aerogel is fragile: say so), self-unfolding by stored elastic energy in 3D-printed or laser-cut living-hinge patterns, shape-memory deployment (pointer; agent 7), unrolling and unspooling (how much ribbon, streamer, thread or paper fits in a thumb tip or a coin: streamer and mouth-coil numbers), liquids and gases as the ultimate compact store (foam from a few millilitres: expanding polyurethane and aerosol foams, shaving foam, soap foam generators, snow polymers such as sodium polyacrylate expanding 40 to 100 times, popcorn, cotton candy, bubbles), growth and inflation hybrids.
- Collapse for vanishes: structures that are rigid until a single trigger and then collapse into a small volume silently (the Zombie gimmick, vanishing cane, vanishing birdcage and vanishing bottle as benchmarks: what do tensegrity, layer jamming with vacuum release, bistable composite, magnetically pinned linkages and inflatables offer instead?), rigid-looking shells that can be crushed or rolled (thin electroformed or vacuum-formed shells: pointer to agent 25), self-retracting systems.
- Mass as the give-away: a deployed object that is far too light, or a stowed load that is too heavy for its container, betrays the method; give densities and what audiences accept (pointer; agent 4).
- Prior art in magic so you can say what is new: appearing and vanishing canes (steel coil and plastic), appearing poles, spring flowers and feather bouquets, production boxes and their load capacity principles, the mouth coil, snowstorm, collapsible birdcages (Abbott's, Tommy Wonder's version and why it is admired), nesting and collapsing props (Walsh cane, appearing straw, appearing 8-foot pole, appearing ladder), folding coins and bill gimmicks, silk-to-cane, production of large objects (bowling ball from a sketch pad: thin shell principle), Zombie ball. For each note the stow ratio and deploy time if you can find them, and the engineering idea that could beat it.

Express results as numbers: stow ratio, deploy time, force or energy, noise, mass, cycles, cost. In "Effect concepts" propose 8 to 12 mechanisms, with at least four that use a speed or compaction capability magic does not currently use.
